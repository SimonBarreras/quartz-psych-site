In psychology a model of memory is a hypothesized representation of representation of memory. Sort of an outline of how and 'where' different stores and processes work according to the most actual evidence. Hence, models change over time.
___

![[Multi-Store Model.png]]

This model was first proposed by [[Atkinson & Shiffrin (1968)]]
Although the model is rather simplistic, they were the first to suggest a basic structure for memory, and sparked much research based on the idea that humans are information processors.

<details markdown='1'><summary>Assumptions</summary> The model is clearly inspired by Computer Science. And is based in multiple assumptions.
- The model argues that memory consists of a number of separate locations where information is stored.
- Memory processes are sequential
- Each memory story operates in a single, uniform way. </details>
___

In this model Short-term Memory (STM) serves as a gateway for information to get to Long-term Memory (LTM). The various memory stores are seen as components that operate in conjunction with the permanent memory store (LTM) through processes such as attention, coding, and rehearsal.

>[!NOTE]
>You need to pay attention in order to remember information.

The Model Suggest that information enters the brain through **Sensory Memory**. Which is **modality specific** (meaning it is related and dependent to different senses such as hearing and vision). The most important stores in the model are the visual store and the auditory store.

>[!NOTE]
>Sensory store has limited capacity and duration,  information only stays here for a few seconds and a very small amount of information goes to STM

The capacity of the STM is believed limited. Initially [[Miller's Magic Number 7 (1956)]] suggested that limit was around 7+/- 2 items and duration was predicted to be normally around 6 to 18 seconds. However rehearsal could facilitate the information to stay for up to 30s in the STM, before being displaced.

The LTM store is conceptualized as a vast storehouse of information, with unknown/indefinite capacity and duration. The information is not stored as a replica of the events or facts but rather as an outline, which means memory can be distorted when retrieved, as we fill in the gaps, as predicted in [[Heuristics - Schema Theory]].

---
**Evidence for this model**

- [[HM (Milner, 1996)]]: Provides evidence on how STM and LTM are located in different stores in the brain.
- [[Glanzer and Cunitz (1966)]]: Used an interference task to prove there are two processes involved in retrieving information. Studied **Primacy effect** and **Recency effect**.

___

**Strengths of the MSM**

- There is significant research to support the theory of separate memory stores - both in experimental research and biological case studies of patients with brain damage.
- The model is of historical importance. It gave psychologists a way to talk about memory and much of the research which followed was based on this model.

**Limitations of the MSM**

- The model is over-simplified. It assumes that each of the stores works as an independent unit.
- The model does not explain memory distortion.
- The model does not explain why some things may be learned with a minimal amount of rehearsal. For example, once bitten by a dog, that memory is quite vivid in spite of the lack of rehearsal.
- There are several times that we rehearse a lot to remember information and it is not transferred to LTM.
