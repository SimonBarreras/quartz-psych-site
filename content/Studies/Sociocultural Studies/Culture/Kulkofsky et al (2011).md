#Study #Sociocultural [[Cultural dimensions]], [[Flashbulb Memory]]
___

**Kulkofsky et al.** conducted a <u>Cross-Cultural Study</u> investigating whether **cultural dimensions**, particularly **individualism vs. collectivism**, affect how people form and recall **flashbulb memories (FBMs)**, examining whether the emotional and social significance of events differs across cultures.
___

**Aim:** To investigate whether culture (specifically individualism vs. collectivism) influences the formation of flashbulb memories (FBMs).

**Participants:** **274 middle class adults** from **5 countries** representing a range on the individualism-collectivism dimension:
- **Individualist cultures**: USA, Germany, UK
- **Collectivist cultures**: China, Turkey

**Method:** <u>Cross-cultural questionnaire / interview study</u>.
- Participants were asked to recall **public events** that occurred during their life time (at least 1yr ago). This was used to create a "memory questionnaire", mirroring the one used in [[Brown & Kulik (1977) - JFK and MLK]], which included details + personal relevance.
- The tools were constructed in english, translated and back-translated into the different languages. 
- They also reported on **social sharing**: how often they discussed the event with others.
- The quality, vividness, and confidence of FBMs were measured and compared across cultures.

**Results:**
- **Individualist cultures** (USA, Germany, UK) tended to have FBMs for a **wider range of events**, including international ones.
- **Collectivist cultures** showed stronger FBMs for events with national or social relevance, compared with **individualistic cultures** that place greater emphasis on an individual's personal involvement and emotional experiences.
- **Social sharing** was a significant predictor of FBM strength across all cultures; the more participants discussed an event, the stronger and more vivid the memory. **Collectivist cultures** however, tend to de-emphasize individual experiences, leading to less rehearsal of the triggering event, and thus less chance of developing FMBs for personal events.

**Conclusion:** The formation and maintenance of flashbulb memories is **culturally influenced**. Social sharing, rehearsing memories through conversation, appears to be a key mechanism.

___

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Cross-cultural design** (5 countries) increases generalizability | **Self-report measures**: vividness and confidence ratings are subjective |
| Identifies **social sharing** as a previously underemphasized mechanism in FBM | Cannot establish whether social sharing **causes** stronger FBMs or results from them |
| Links cognitive psychology (FBM) to **sociocultural factors** | Events tested may not be equally significant across all cultures |
| Challenges purely biological (Brown & Kulik "Now Print!") accounts of FBM | **Sampling issues**: participants may not be representative of their national culture |
| Advances understanding of **cultural cognition** | Difficult to fully operationalize "collectivism" at the individual level |

> **Key Concept - Social Sharing & Memory:**
> *Kulkofsky et al. highlights that memory is not purely an individual, biological process. Social sharing, talking about events with others, actively reinforces and shapes memories. This is especially relevant in collectivist cultures where communal storytelling and group remembering are integral social practices.*
