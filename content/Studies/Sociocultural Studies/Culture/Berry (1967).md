#Study #Sociocultural [[Cultural dimensions]], [[Conformity]]
___

**Berry (1967)** conducted a <u>Cross-Cultural Quasi-Experiment</u> investigating whether **conformity behaviour** differs across cultures, specifically comparing cultures that are highly interdependent (collectivist) with those that are more independent (individualist), extending Asch's conformity paradigm cross-culturally.

[[Hofstede (2001)]]; Individualism-Collectivism dimension. Although happened before the theory. 

Based on <u>Barry, Child, And bacon (1959)</u>, which suggested that hunting and fishing societies tend to be individualistic, whereas agricultural societies tend to be collectivistic.
___

**Aim:** To investigate whether levels of conformity in a perceptual line-matching task (Asch paradigm) differ between cultures that vary in their degree of **collectivism vs. individualism** and their reliance on visual vs. ecological cues.

**Participants:** 3(120) Samples from two contrasting cultures and a control:
- **Temne people of Sierra Leone**: a highly interdependent, collectivist agricultural society where conformity is culturally valued and essential for survival.
- **Inuit (Eskimo) people of Baffin Island, Canada**: a more independent, individualist hunter-gatherer society where independent judgement is essential for survival.
- Urban and Rural Scottish people were also tested as a Western comparison group.

**Method:** <u>Cross-cultural quasi-experiment</u> using an adapted version of **Asch's line-matching task**.
- Directions were given in the participant's native language
- Participants were shown a standard line and asked to match it to one of several comparison lines.
- For the 3 trial, the researcher gave a "hint" saying people of their own culture selected the "this"(correct) line. 
- For trials 4-6 the wrong answer was hinted (five lines away, total 8)
- Conformity was measured by whether the participant agreed with the incorrect majority.

**Results:**
- **Temne participants** (collectivists) showed **significantly higher conformity** rates than Inuit participants, even though it was incorrect. 
- **Inuit participants** showed **lower conformity**, even lower than the control group. 
- No difference within groups

**Conclusion:** Berry interpreted the difference as reflecting the ecological demands of each culture. This supports the role of **cultural dimensions** (individualism vs. collectivism) in shaping cognitive and social behaviour.

## Evaluation

| Strengths                                                                                                                             | Limitations                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cross-cultural design** directly tests universality of conformity                                                                   | **Small, specific: difficult to generalize beyond the two cultures studied. + Scotts do not represent the general population.                                                     |
| Uses a **standardized task** (Asch paradigm) for comparability                                                                        | **Low ecological validity**: The Asch paradigm is a **Western, lab-based** task that may be culturally biased.                                                                    |
| Supports the role of **ecological factors** in shaping social behaviour                                                               | Conformity may reflect **demand characteristics** or unfamiliarity with the task                                                                                                  |
| Challenges the universality of conformity findings from Asch (1951)                                                                   | **Causality cannot be determined:** Quasi-experiment, thus participants weren't randomly allocated to conditions. However, control condition helps recover some Internal validity |
| Links to **Hofstede's cultural dimensions** (individualism-collectivism axis)                                                         | Risk of **cultural stereotyping**: individuals within cultures vary enormously                                                                                                    |
| **Etic:** Used standardized procedure, making it highly replicable and reliable. + The test was administered in their native language | **Temporal validity:** Old, less globalized world. Is it still valid today?                                                                                                       |
|                                                                                                                                       | **Ecological Fallacy:** Believing that since an individual is a member of one of these groups, he would be more or less likely to conform. (can lead to stereotyping)             |

> **Key Concept - Individualism vs. Collectivism:**
> *Individualist cultures (e.g., USA, UK) prioritize personal autonomy and independent thought. Collectivist cultures (e.g., many Asian and African societies) prioritize group harmony and interdependence. Berry (1967) showed that this cultural dimension predicts real behavioural differences in conformity, one of the first empirical tests of cross-cultural variation in social behaviour.*
