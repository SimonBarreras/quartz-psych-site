#Study #Sociocultural [[Cultural dimensions]]
___

**Hofstede** conducted a <u>Large-Scale Cross-Cultural Survey</u> identifying the key **dimensions along which national cultures differ**, providing one of the most widely used frameworks for understanding cultural variation in psychology, business, and social science.
___

**Aim:** To identify and quantify the key dimensions along which national cultures differ in values, attitudes, and behaviours, and to map countries along these dimensions.

**Participants:** Over **116,000 IBM employees** across **40 most represented countries** in the original surveys, collected between 1967-1973. Hofstede (2001) extended this with scores over 30 years, claiming results stayed consistent across time.

**Method:** <u>Large-scale questionnaire survey</u> with factor analysis.
- Employees answered questions about their **work-related values**, preferences for management styles, attitudes toward hierarchy, and social norms.
- Hofstede carried out a **factor analysis** on the responses, focusing on key differences submitted by employees in different countries to identify underlying **cultural dimensions**.
- The research is **etic** in approach: applying a standardized framework across cultures rather than studying each culture from within.

**The Cultural Dimensions:**

| Dimension | Description |
|---|---|
| **Individualism vs. Collectivism (IDV)** | Degree to which individuals are integrated into groups; prioritizing personal goals vs. group harmony |
| **Power Distance (PDI)** | Acceptance of unequal distribution of power in institutions and organizations |
| **Uncertainty Avoidance (UAI)** | Tolerance for ambiguity and uncertainty; preference for rules and structure |
| **Masculinity vs. Femininity (MAS)** | Emphasis on competition, achievement, and assertiveness vs. cooperation, modesty, and quality of life |
| **Long-Term vs. Short-Term Orientation (LTO)** | Focus on future rewards and perseverance vs. respect for tradition and immediate results |
| **Indulgence vs. Restraint** *(added later)* | Freedom to enjoy leisure vs. social norms suppressing gratification |

**Key Findings:**
- **USA** scored highest on individualism. **Guatemala** and many Asian countries scored highest on collectivism.
- High **power distance** cultures (e.g., Malaysia, Philippines) accept hierarchies more readily than low PD cultures (e.g., Denmark, Austria).
- **Japan** scored highest on uncertainty avoidance and masculinity.

**Conclusion:** National cultures can be meaningfully compared along measurable psychological dimensions. These dimensions predict real behavioural differences in social behaviour, conformity, health, education, and more.

___

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Enormous sample size** (116,000+) and breadth (40+ countries); scores consistent over 30 years | **IBM employees** are not representative of their national populations (corporate, educated); attitudes toward work may not transfer to all areas of daily life |
| Provides a **standardized, quantifiable framework** for cross-cultural comparison | **Ecological fallacy**: country-level dimensions may not apply to individuals within that culture |
| Highly **influential**: widely used in psychology, anthropology, business, and medicine | Cultures change over time; data from the 1960s-70s may not reflect current values |
| Supports research on **individualism-collectivism** differences in memory, conformity, and health | Dimensions may reflect **Western conceptual biases** in how culture is measured |
| **Factor analysis** used to identify dimensions; team-based coding established inter-rater reliability across researchers | **Researcher bias** in factor analysis: researchers decide which correlations are significant; risk of confirming their own assumptions |

## IB Connections

- **Approach**: Sociocultural
- **Topic**: Cultural dimensions; Social and cultural influences on behaviour
- **Key Concept**: Individualism vs. collectivism; power distance; cultural dimensions framework
- **Useful for**: *"Describe cultural dimensions and their influence on behaviour"* / *"Evaluate cross-cultural research methods"*
- **Paired with**: Berry (1967), Kulkofsky et al., Bartlett (1932), Steele & Aronson (1995)

___

> **Key Concept - Individualism vs. Collectivism (Most IB-Relevant Dimension):**
> *The IDV dimension is the most frequently cited in IB Psychology. Individualist cultures (high IDV: USA, UK, Australia) prioritize personal goals, autonomy, and self-expression. Collectivist cultures (low IDV: China, Guatemala, South Korea) prioritize group loyalty, harmony, and interdependence. This dimension predicts differences in conformity (Berry, 1967), memory sharing (Kulkofsky et al.), and many other behaviours studied in IB Psychology.*
