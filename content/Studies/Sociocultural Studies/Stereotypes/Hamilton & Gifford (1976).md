#Study #Sociocultural [[Stereotypes]], [[Heuristics - Schema Theory]]
___

**Hamilton and Gifford** conducted a <u>Lab Experiment</u> demonstrating that **illusory correlation** can cause stereotypes to form purely through cognitive mechanisms, without any prior prejudice or real group differences.

**Background:** An **illusory correlation** is the tendency to perceive a relationship between two variables when none exists. It is rooted in the **availability heuristic** (System 1 thinking): when minority group membership and negative behaviour are both statistically rare, their co-occurrence is distinctive and disproportionately memorable, leading people to overestimate the association.

**Aim:** To investigate whether a distinctiveness-based illusory correlation can produce stereotypic beliefs about a minority group in the absence of any real difference between groups.

**Participants:** **40 American undergraduates** (20 male, 20 female).

**Method:** <u>Lab experiment</u> with repeated measures design.
- Participants were shown slides describing behaviours performed by members of two fictional groups:
  - **Group A**: 26 members (majority)
  - **Group B**: 13 members (minority)
- Behaviours were either desirable or undesirable, in the same ratio for both groups:

| | Desirable | Undesirable | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group A | 18 | 8 | 26 |
| Group B | 9 | 4 | 13 |

- Afterwards, participants rated each group on positive and negative traits and estimated how many undesirable behaviours each group had performed.

**Results:**
- Participants recalled more positive traits for Group A (74%) than Group B (54%).
- Participants recalled more negative traits for Group B (65%) than Group A (55%).
- Group B was rated more negatively overall, despite the two groups being objectively identical in their ratio of positive to negative behaviours.

**Conclusion:** Stereotypes can form through purely cognitive mechanisms. The co-occurrence of minority group membership and negative behaviour was disproportionately salient, leading participants to develop a negative stereotype of Group B with no real basis.

**Link to question:** This demonstrates the **cognitive formation of stereotypes** through the illusory correlation heuristic, without prejudice, conflict, or social learning.

---

> **Key Concept - Illusory Correlation:**
> *When two things are both rare, their co-occurrence is cognitively salient and easier to recall, making it feel as though they are related even when they are not. This is sufficient to produce negative stereotypes about minority groups from statistical rarity alone.*
