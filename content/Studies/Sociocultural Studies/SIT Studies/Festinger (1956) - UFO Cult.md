#Study #SIT  [[Observations]], [[Ethical considerations]]
___

**Festinger** carried out a <u>Participant Naturalistic Observation</u> with the aim of studying **cognitive dissonance** within a small UFO doomsday cult called "the seekers".
___

Cult Members believed a great flood was approaching and only "true believers" would be rescued. 
Festinger wanted to study the <u>reaction of people in the cult</u> after finding their belief (Doomsday event) was in fact not true; and the <u>foot-in-door paradigm</u> (studies the level of commitment exhibited by members).
___
**Aim:** Festinger wanted to find out how the members of the cult would cope when they realized that this apocalyptic event did not happen.

The goal of the observations was to determine the degree to which each member was convinced of the truth of the belief system. **Foot-in-the-door** **paradigm**; level of commitment exhibited by members. 

The theory was that the more of a commitment they had made, the less likely they were to change their behavior, even despite a major disappointment.

**Participants:** UFO cult "*the seekers*"

**Procedure:** Covert participant naturalistic observation. 
	- Festinger learned about the prophecy via a news paper and train a team of 8 observers for a month
	- The cult was located in two homes 
	- Observers pretended to be true believers
	- Observers were students or staff members in the department of psychology and had experience in interviewing and observation techniques
	- Observes documented what actions had and would be taken as the flood approached
	- 
	

**Results:** According to Festinger, they rationalized the situation in a way that maintained their sense of self-esteem.

Cognitive dissonance; they invested so much into this belief,  that the predictions turning out to be wrong, led to extreme anxiety. One way to alleviate this anxiety was to justify their behavior (accepting the message given by their leader). 

**Evaluation:**
	- There are concerns with regard to the observation as there was no regular pattern of activities, and the participants didn't live in the cult houses. They only attended meetings there. 
	- The observers were not informed until only a few days before the meetings would take place. 
	- Taking notes was vary difficult and they would have to rely on memory 3-4h after the meetings.
	- The study was the first of its type 
	- Supported theory on **Cognitive dissonance**
	- Ethical concern; Trust
	- Stories told by observers to gain access to the cult, validated their beliefs + participant observations may allow the observer to influence participants behaviour. 
	- Observers reported having other duties, mental and physical fatigue, which may have affected the way data was recorded
	- No standardized tool of assessment / No measurement of strength of beliefs a few days before/after the event
