**Bartlett** conducted a <u>Series of Memory Experiments</u> demonstrating that memory is reconstructive and influenced by **cultural schemas**, challenging the view that memory is a passive, accurate recording of events.

> **Useful for:** *"Discuss/evaluate schema theory"* / *"To what extent is one cognitive process reliable?"* / *"Discuss cultural influences on cognitive processes"*

Bartlett was one of the first to show how cultural schema influence remembering. He found that people had trouble remembering a story from another culture and that they reconstructed it to fit in with their own cultural schemas.
___

**Aim:** To investigate how previous knowledge (e.g., cultural schemas) affects the encoding and recall of a culturally unfamiliar story, and to demonstrate that memory is an active, reconstructive process.

**Participants:** **20 British adults**, a narrow, culturally homogeneous sample (limitation: cultural bias in schema background).

**Method:** <u>Experiment</u> using two recall techniques.

Participants were presented the **"The War of the Ghosts"**, a Native American folk tale with unfamiliar cultural elements, and an unfamiliar structure. 

Two recall methods were used:
- **Serial reproduction**: Participant 1 recalls the story to Participant 2, who recalls it to Participant 3, and so on (like Chinese whispers).
- **Repeated reproduction**: The same participant recalls the story multiple times over a  short period of  time, days, weeks, months or years.

**Results:**
- There was no significant difference between the way that the groups recalled the story. 

| Phenomenon                  | Description                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Levelling**               | The story became **shorter** with each reproduction; unfamiliar or culturally incongruent details were omitted                                                    |
| **Sharpening**              | Certain details were retained and **emphasized** more prominently                                                                                                 |
| **Rationalisation**         | Supernatural elements (e.g., "something black came out of his mouth") were reinterpreted to fit Western schemas, made more logical and familiar or "conventional" |
| **(Cultural) Assimilation** | The story was unconsciously altered to fit cultural expectations.                                                                                                 |

**Conclusion:** Memory is not a literal replay of events but a **reconstruction** shaped by existing **schemas** (organized knowledge structures). When encountering culturally unfamiliar material, people unconsciously modify memories to match their existing cultural framework.

___

## Evaluation

| Strengths                                                                                       | Limitations                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pioneering study**: first major demonstration that memory is reconstructive                   | **Methodologically weak**: no standardized procedure; conditions varied across participants                                                                                                                            |
| Identified specific, named distortion effects (levelling, sharpening, rationalisation)          | **Cannot establish cause-and-effect**: unclear whether distortions were due to schemas, forgetting, or imagination + no IV was manipulated while other variables held constant to observe systematic effect on some DV |
| Results have been **replicated cross-culturally** and extended by [[Brewer & Treyers (1981) - "Schema congruent" office]]   | **Small, non-representative sample**: 20 British adults                                                                                                                                                                |
| Practically relevant; applications in education, eyewitness testimony, and clinical psychology  | The story was intentionally unusual, which may exaggerate distortions compared to everyday memory                                                                                                                      |
| Introduced the concept of **schema** to psychology; hugely influential theoretical contribution | Bartlett did not use standardized scoring, making subjective interpretation of distortions a concern                                                                                                                   |
| Natural setting: reading a story                                                                | Failure of replication by other studies - low reliability e.g.,  Bergman & Roedeger (1999)                                                                                                                             |

> **Key Concept - Schema Theory:**
> *A schema is a mental framework of prior knowledge that helps us organise and interpret new information. Bartlett (1932) showed that when memory conflicts with existing schemas, it is unconsciously altered to fit, a process called rationalisation. This is why memory is not a faithful record but an active reconstruction, always shaped by what we already know and expect.*
