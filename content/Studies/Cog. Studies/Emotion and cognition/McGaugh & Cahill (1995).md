#study  #cognitive #Biological  [[Flashbulb Memory]], [[Hormones In Human Behaviour]]-Adrenaline
____

**McGaugh and Cahill** conducted a <u>Double-Blind Experiment</u> investigating whether **adrenaline (epinephrine)** and **emotional arousal** enhance the consolidation of long-term memories, using pharmacological manipulation to test the role of hormones in memory.
___

**Back ground information**: In a traumatic event the physiological stress system automatically gets going; our **"fight or flight"** response. This is because stress hormones like adrenaline and cortisol are released from the adrenal gland, causing activation of de amygdala to signal the occurrence of something important or dangerous. It appears that the amygdala attaches emotional significance to otherwise neutral stimuli.   

**Aim:** To investigate the role of emotional arousal (via adrenaline release) in memory consolidation. 

**Participants:** Healthy adult volunteers randomly assigned to experimental and control conditions.

**Method:** <u>Double-blind, placebo-controlled experiment</u>.
- Participants were divided into **two groups** and were presented either a boring or an emotionally arousing story
	  - **Story 1** (neutral): A mother and son visit the father at a hospital and witness a simulated drill .
	  - **Story 2** (emotionally arousing): The son is injured in a car accident and undergoes surgery. + happy ending / recovery
- Participants rated *"how emotional they found the story" (1-10)
- Two week later, participants completed a **memory test** for story details (multiple choice).

**Follow-up (1996)**:
- Procedure above was repeated
- Participants received either **propranolol** (beta-blocker) or a **placebo** before hearing a story.
>[!NOTE]
>Beta-blockers are used to inhibit target cells for adrenalin so that the heart will pump more slowly and efficiently. However, in this study it was used to prevent activation of the amygdala. 

**Results:**
- **Original study**: The emotional story lead to better overall recall of the story. 
- Participants who received the **placebo** showed significantly better memory for the emotionally arousing story compared to the experimental condition.
- Participants who received **propranolol** showed remembered details no better than those who heard the "mundane" story. 

**Conclusion:** The study concluded that the amygdala plays a significant role in the creation of memories linked to emotional arousal, and linked this to the "special biological mechanism" for FBMs that Brown & kulik had theorized. 

Emotional arousal enhances memory consolidation through the release of **adrenaline**, which acts on the **amygdala** to strengthen long-term memory encoding. Blocking adrenaline eliminates this enhancement.

## Evaluation

| Strengths                                                                                                                                                        | Limitations                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Double-blind design** eliminates bias from both experimenter and participant                                                                                   | **Artificial lab setting**: story-based arousal differs from real-life emotional trauma ∴ **Low ecological validity**. Results may not generalize to all types of emotional events |
| Established **Cause and effect relationship:** Pharmacological control (propranolol) isolates adrenaline and the activation of the amygdala as the key variable. | **Ethical concerns**: administering drugs (propranolol) to healthy participants. + Deception                                                                                       |
| **Applicable and replicable** (standardized): Research has been applied on the treatment of PTSD patients (Pitman et al, 2002)                                   | Cannot fully control for individual differences in baseline emotional reactivity                                                                                                   |
| Supports  Brown & kulik's theory: the **amygdala** in emotionally significant memories as the mechanism for FBMs                                                 | The recognition task, being a 3-choice test may not be a valid test of memory.                                                                                                     |
|                                                                                                                                                                  |                                                                                                                                                                                    |

> **Key Concept - Adrenaline & Memory Consolidation:**
> *During emotionally significant events, the body releases adrenaline (epinephrine). This hormone activates the amygdala, which then modulates the hippocampus to strengthen memory encoding, a process called emotional memory consolidation. McGaugh & Cahill (1995) showed that blocking adrenaline with propranolol eliminates this enhancement, confirming the biological basis of emotional memory.*
