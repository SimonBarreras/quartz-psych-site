___

**Sharot et al.** conducted a <u>Correlational Study using fMRI</u> to investigate whether people who were physically close to the World Trade Center on 9/11 formed stronger **flashbulb memories**, and whether these memories were associated with distinct **hippocampal and amygdala activation**.
___

**Aim:** Investigate whether personal significance (e.g., due to physical proximity) of a traumatic event (9/11) influences the formation of flashbulb memories, and identify potential brain factors involved.

**Participants:** **24 New Yorkers** who were in Manhattan on September 11, 2001. Recruited through advertisements (voluntary sampling). Participants provided informed consent and <u>were compensated for their participation</u>. 

**Method:** <u>Quasi-experiment</u>. Correlational study with fMRI neuroimaging.
- **3 years after 9/11**, participants were shown word cues related to:
	  - **The 9/11 attacks**
	  - **A control event** (e.g., a celebrity news story)
	The word was accompanied by the word "Summer" or "September" for them to link the word to their holidays or 9/11 
- Participants brain activity was observed while recalling the events via  **fMRI scanning.**
- Participants were asked to rate their memories for vividness, detail, accuracy, confidence and arousal. And to write a description of their personal memories. 
- Proximity to Ground Zero was recorded and correlated with memory strength and neural activity.

**Results:**
- Only half of the participants were found to have "FMBs" (greater sense of detail and confidence in the accuracy of their memories)
- 9/11 memories activated the **amygdala** more strongly than control memories.
- Participants who were **closer to Ground Zero** had **more vivid and confident** memories of 9/11 and showed greater amygdala activation than those who were farther away. 
- The strength of amygdala activation was shown to correlate with FBMs 

 Close personal experience may be critical for the formation the activation of neural mechanisms (involving the amygdala) that produce the vivid memories related to FMBs 
 This study provides **neurobiological evidence** for the formation of FBMs, supporting the role of emotional arousal in memory encoding.

## Evaluation

| Strengths                                                                         | Limitations                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **fMRI provides objective neuroimaging data**, not reliant on self-report alone   | **Correlational**: proximity is not manipulated; cannot confirm cause-and-effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Bridges **cognitive and biological** approaches effectively                       | **Small sample and culturally biased** (24 participants) limits statistical power and generalizability + participant variability (Individual differences in trauma response are not controlled for). Research indicates that individualistic cultures are more likely to have FMBs. This makes generalizability low.  |
| Real-life traumatic event increases **ecological validity**                       | Memories were tested **3 years later**, making it impossible to verify against original encoding                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Identifies **specific brain structures** (amygdala, hippocampus) in FBM formation | Ethical sensitivity of using 9/11 as a stimulus                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Supports and extends **McGaugh & Cahill** (adrenaline-amygdala link)              | Although the study demonstrates the role of the amygdala as a result of proximity to the event, it does not explain why some people have vivid memories after seeing the events on television or the Internet.                                                                                                        |

> **Key Concept - Amygdala & Emotional Memory:**
> *The amygdala is central to emotional processing and plays a key role in flagging emotionally significant events for enhanced encoding. Sharot et al. (2007) provides direct neuroimaging evidence that the amygdala and hippocampus work together to produce flashbulb memories, with proximity to trauma strengthening this effect.*
