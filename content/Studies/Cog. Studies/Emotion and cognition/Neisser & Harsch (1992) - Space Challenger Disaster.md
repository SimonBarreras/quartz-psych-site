#Study  #Cultural #cognitive [[Flashbulb Memory]]
___

**Neisser and Harsch** conducted a <u>Longitudinal Self-Report Study</u> to investigate whether **flashbulb memories** are truly accurate, using the 1986 Space Shuttle Challenger disaster as the triggering event.
___

**Aim:** To investigate whether flashbulb memories are as accurate and stable as people believe them to be, by measuring memory for the Challenger disaster immediately after the event and again 2.5 years later.

**Participants:** Original sample was 106, but suffered attrition to **44 students** at Emory University (USA) who completed both phases of the study.

**Method:** <u>Longitudinal self-report study</u>. <u>Case study</u>
- The **morning after** the Challenger explosion (January 29, 1986), participants filled out a questionnaire describing where they were, who told them, what they were doing, and their emotional reaction.
- **2.5 years later**, the same participants filled in an identical questionnaire from memory.
- Responses were scored for accuracy (0-7 scale) and compared to their original accounts.
- Participants were also asked to rate their **confidence** (0-5) in the accuracy of their memory.
- Participants were asked if they remembered filling out the questionnaire before
- Semi-structured interviews were carried out a few months later. 

**Results:**
- Accuracy scores were **very low**: The mean score was 2.95/07, 33 scored between 0-2, and only 3 scored 7. 
- **25% of participants** recalled doing the questionnaire originally.  
- Despite poor accuracy, participants showed **high confidence** in the correctness of their memories. (avg. 4.17)
- When shown their original accounts, several participants insisted their new (inaccurate) memory was the correct one.
- For the most part participants maintained their original story 2.5 years later. Additional cues had little effect

**Conclusion:** Flashbulb memories are **not reliably accurate** despite feeling vivid and certain. The study challenges Brown & Kulik's (1977) claim that FBMs are a special, accurate form of memory, suggesting instead that vividness does not equal accuracy.

## Evaluation

| Strengths                                                                                                                                                                      | Limitations                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Longitudinal design** allows direct comparison of original vs. later memory                                                                                                  | **Participant Attrition** (44 students) limits generalizability                                                                                                                             |
| Original questionnaire provides an **objective baseline** for accuracy                                                                                                         | **American university students** only; low cultural representativeness                                                                                                                      |
| Directly tests and **challenges Brown & Kulik's (1977)** FBM theory                                                                                                            | Cannot fully control for rehearsal effects (discussing the event over 2.5 years)                                                                                                            |
| Highlights the **metacognitive failure** of overconfidence in memory                                                                                                           | Only one triggering event; cannot generalize to all FBM contexts                                                                                                                            |
| Supports **reconstructive memory** theories (Bartlett, 1932)                                                                                                                   | The delay of 2.5 years is long; more recent follow-ups at shorter intervals might show different results                                                                                    |
| **Strong method**: Prospective, **Method triangulation**; Questionnaire (same as B&K and Intvw) and High ecological validity; The study was performed under natural conditions | However; no control over confounding variables between questionnaires (e.g., rehearsal)                                                                                                     |
| Research base and follow up studies demonstrate **transferability** of findings                                                                                                | Demand characteristics; Social desirability bias could explain high confidence levels. (avoid social disapproval for claiming not to remember an important day or pleasing the researchers) |

> **Key Concept - Vividness does not equal Accuracy:**
> *Neisser & Harsch (1992) is the critical counter-study to Brown & Kulik. FBMs feel clear and permanent, but this confidence is misleading. Memory is reconstructive (Bartlett, 1932), and even emotionally significant memories are subject to distortion and error over time.*
