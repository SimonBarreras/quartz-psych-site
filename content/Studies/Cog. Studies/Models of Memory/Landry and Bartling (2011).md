#Study #cognitive [[Working Memory Model]], [[Articulatory Suppression]]
___

**Landry and Bartling** conducted a <u>Lab Experiment</u> testing the **Working Memory Model** by using **articulatory suppression** to disrupt the **phonological loop**, demonstrating that this component plays a specific and necessary role in verbal serial recall.
___

**Aim:** To investigate whether articulatory suppression (a concurrent verbal task) impairs the serial recall of a written list of phonologically dissimilar letters, as predicted by the phonological loop component of Baddeley & Hitch's Working Memory Model.

**Participants:** **34 undergraduate psychology students**, randomly assigned to two independent groups.

**Method:** <u>Lab experiment</u> with independent groups design.

| Group | Task | Articulatory suppression? |
|---|---|---|
| **Control** | Memorize letter list | No - silent memorization |
| **Experimental** | Memorize letter list | Yes - repeatedly say "1, 2" at 2 per second |

**Stimuli:** 10 lists of **7 letters** each, drawn from F, K, L, M, R, X, Q, chosen because they are **phonologically dissimilar** (do not sound alike), to control for phonological similarity effects.

**Procedure:**
- Each list was displayed for **5 seconds**, followed by a **5-second wait**, then written recall on an answer sheet.
- Recall was scored as correct only if the letter was in the **correct serial position**.
- Average percentage correct recall was calculated for each participant.

**Results:**

| Group | Mean % Correct Recall | SD |
|---|---|---|
| **Control** | **76%** | 0.13 |
| **Experimental** (articulatory suppression) | **45%** | 0.14 |

- A **t-test** confirmed the difference was statistically significant at **p < 0.01**.
- Standard deviations were nearly identical across groups, indicating consistent within-group performance.

**Conclusion:** Articulatory suppression significantly impaired serial recall of verbal material. Because the suppression task (saying "1, 2") occupied the **phonological loop's articulatory rehearsal process**, participants in the experimental group could not rehearse the letter strings, leading to substantially lower accuracy. This directly supports the WMM's prediction that the phonological loop is essential for verbal working memory.

___

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Controlled experiment** with random assignment; high internal validity | **Artificial task**: memorizing random letter strings has low ecological validity |
| Clear **operationalization** of articulatory suppression as the IV | **Small sample** (34 students) limits generalizability |
| Statistical analysis (t-test, SD) adds **quantitative rigour** | Participants were all psychology undergraduates; potential demand characteristics |
| Directly and specifically tests the **phonological loop** component of the WMM | Only tested one component of WMM; does not address central executive or visuospatial sketchpad |
| Results align precisely with **Baddeley & Hitch's (1974)** theoretical predictions | Task may not represent how phonological loop functions in real-world multitasking |

> **Key Concept - Articulatory Suppression:**
> *Articulatory suppression is a technique where participants are made to repeat an irrelevant sound (e.g., "1, 2, 1, 2") continuously. This occupies the articulatory rehearsal process of the phonological loop, preventing it from being used to rehearse other verbal material. Landry & Bartling (2011) uses this to isolate the phonological loop and confirm its specific, necessary role in verbal working memory.*
