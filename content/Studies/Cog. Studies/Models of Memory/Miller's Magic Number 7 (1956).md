#Study #cognitive [[Multi-Store Model of Memory (MSM)]]
___

**George Miller** published a theoretical paper proposing the **"Magic Number 7, Plus or Minus Two"** as the capacity limit of **short-term memory (STM)**, one of the most cited papers in psychology and a cornerstone of the Multi-Store Model.

> Note: Miller (1956) is primarily a **theoretical/review paper**, not a single controlled experiment. It reviewed and synthesized multiple studies on STM capacity.

___

**Aim:** To review existing research on the capacity of short-term memory and propose a unifying principle for its limits.

**Key Proposal - The Magic Number 7 (+/- 2):**
- Miller reviewed studies on **absolute judgement** (distinguishing stimuli on a single dimension) and **immediate memory span** (recalling sequences of items).
- He found that human performance in both tasks is limited to approximately **7 items** (range: 5-9).
- He coined the term **"chunks"**, units of meaningful information, arguing that STM holds **7 +/- 2 chunks**, not individual bits of information.
- **Chunking** allows people to expand STM capacity by grouping information: e.g., "FBI" is 3 letters but 1 chunk.

**Supporting the MSM:**
- Miller's finding directly supported Atkinson & Shiffrin's (1968) claim that **STM has limited capacity**.
- It also explained why phone numbers, postcodes, and passport numbers tend to be 5-9 digits long.

___

## Challenge: Cowan (2001)

**Nelson Cowan** argued that Miller's 7 +/- 2 estimate was **too optimistic**, because Miller's tasks allowed participants to use **rehearsal and grouping strategies** that artificially inflated capacity.

Using a **"running span" procedure** (participants did not know when recall would be tested), Cowan found true STM capacity to be only **4 +/- 1 chunks**, not 7.

**Biological support (Vogel & Machizawa, 2004):**
- fMRI studies show activity in the **parietal cortex** increases with each additional item held in STM, but levels off at **approximately 4 items**, consistent with Cowan's lower estimate.

___

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Highly influential**: shaped decades of memory research and the MSM | Miller's tasks allowed **chunking and rehearsal strategies**, artificially inflating the true capacity estimate |
| Concept of **chunking** is practically useful (education, interface design) | Paper was a **review/theoretical synthesis**, not a single controlled experiment |
| Consistent with the **MSM's** claim of limited STM capacity | **Cowan (2001)** and neuroimaging data suggest the real limit is closer to **4 chunks** |
| Applies to a wide range of memory tasks (digits, letters, words, tones) | Does not address **duration** of STM, only capacity |
| Real-world applicability (phone numbers, codes, PIN design) | Capacity varies with **type of material** (verbal vs. visual) and individual differences |

> **Key Concept - Chunking:**
> *A chunk is a single meaningful unit of information. STM does not store raw bits; it stores chunks. By reorganizing information into meaningful units (e.g., remembering "IB DP PSY" as 3 chunks rather than 9 letters), we can hold more information within STM's limited capacity. Miller's insight into chunking remains one of the most practically applicable findings in cognitive psychology.*
