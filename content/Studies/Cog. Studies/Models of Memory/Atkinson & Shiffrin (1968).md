#Study #cognitive   [[Multi-Store Model of Memory (MSM)]]
___

> *Note: The MSM was originally published in **1968**. Always cite as Atkinson & Shiffrin (1968) in exams.*

**Richard Atkinson and Richard Shiffrin** proposed the **Multi-Store Model of Memory (MSM)** in **1968**, one of the most influential theoretical frameworks in cognitive psychology. Rather than a single empirical study, it is a **theoretical model** supported by multiple experiments.
___

**Aim / Background:** To propose a structural model explaining how information flows through different memory stores, and how it is encoded, stored, and retrieved.

**The Three Memory Stores:**

| Store | Capacity | Duration | Encoding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sensory Memory** | Very large | Very brief (0.5-3 sec) | Modality-specific (iconic, echoic) |
| **Short-Term Memory (STM)** | Limited (7 +/- 2 items; Miller, 1956) | ~18-30 seconds without rehearsal | Mainly acoustic |
| **Long-Term Memory (LTM)** | Unlimited | Potentially lifetime | Mainly semantic |

**How it works:**
- Information enters through the **sensory register**; most is lost immediately.
- If **attended to**, information passes to **STM**.
- With **rehearsal** (maintenance or elaborative), information transfers to **LTM**.
- Without rehearsal, STM information is lost through **decay or displacement**.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- **Miller (1956)**: STM capacity of 7 +/- 2 items
- **Glanzer & Cunitz (1966)**: primacy and recency effects support separate STM/LTM stores
- **HM case study (Milner, 1966)**: intact LTM but damaged STM-to-LTM transfer
- **KF case study (Warrington & Shallice, 1970)**: damaged STM but intact LTM

___

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Empirically supported** by multiple studies (Miller, Glanzer & Cunitz, HM, KF) | **Oversimplified**: treats STM and LTM as single, unitary stores |
| Clear, testable model; highly **influential** in memory research | **Rehearsal** is not the only way to move information to LTM (e.g., emotional significance) |
| Explains **amnesia and brain damage** findings (HM, KF) | Ignores the **active, reconstructive** nature of memory (Bartlett, 1932) |
| Laid the groundwork for **Baddeley & Hitch's Working Memory Model** (1974) | Cannot explain **procedural memory** or implicit learning |
| Influential in real-world applications (education, therapy) | The role of **attention** in transfer is underspecified |

> **Key Concept - The Multi-Store Model:**
> *The MSM was revolutionary for framing memory as a flow of information through structurally distinct stores. Its key insight, that STM and LTM are separate systems with different properties, is well-supported by neuropsychological evidence (HM, KF). However, it was later refined by the Working Memory Model, which replaced the idea of a passive STM with a more complex, active multi-component system.*
