**Anchoring bias** occurs when we rely too heavily on an initial piece of information offered (considered to be the "anchor") when making decisions.

In the courtroom, often a sentence is recommended by prosecutors.  
Englich and Mussweiler wanted to know if the request for a certain prison sentence would influence the decision made by a judge. They conducted a <u>True Experiment</u> investigating the **anchoring bias** in legal sentencing decisions and demonstrated that judges are influenced by anchors when assigning sentences, a key demonstration of **System 1 thinking**. 
___

**Aim:**  Investigate whether the sentencing recommendations made by prosecutors before trials influence sentencing decisions made by judges by setting a numerical anchor (suggested sentence), which biases their DM. 

**Participants:** 19 Young trial judges, (15 male, 4 female; Avg age 29.4, and Avg experience 9.3 months)

**Method:** <u>True experiment</u> with independent samples design (**ISD**).
- Judges were given a briefing on a case of alleged rape.
- To develop the case materials, advice was sought from highly experienced judges, and tested on a group of 24 senior law students. (pilot study) - Law students recommended a sentence of 17.21 months on avg. (used as a basis to set the anchors)
- Participants were given the case materials as well as copies of the penal code. 
- After 15 minutes of reading the information they were given a **questionnaire**, and the prosecutor demanded a sentence of either 2 months vs. 34 months. 
	The questionnaire asked; 
	- Do you think the requested sentence was low, adequate, or high? 
	- What sentence would you recommend?
	- How certain are you about your recommendation? (1-9)
	- How realistic do you think this case is? (1-9)

**Results:** (avg. rating of **certainty** for pilot group was 7.17/9, SD 1.3)
- **Low anchor**: Judges recommended a sentence of Avg 18.78 months, SD 9.11
- **High anchor**: Judges recommended a sentence of Avg 28.70 months, SD 6.53

**Conclusion:** Legal sentencing decisions are susceptible to **anchoring bias**. A  numerical anchor influenced expert judgement in a context where it should have had zero effect. This demonstrates that **System 1 thinking** influences even trained professionals, and can override the analytical reasoning (System 2) that expertise is supposed to provide.

## Evaluation

| Strengths                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Limitations                                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Professional sample** (real judges) increases ecological validity and makes the finding more meaningful                                                                                                  | **Sample bias**: The sample is small, gender and culturally biased, making it difficult to generalize the findings.       |
| **ISD**: Allowed researchers to use the same case for both conditions. "standardizing", thus increasing reliability.                                                                                       | **ISD**: Means participant variability may have influenced the findings of the study, serving as a confounding variable.  |
| **Realistic case materials**: Directly demonstrates anchoring in a high-stakes domain using real judges on a realistic case. Has significant policy implications for how legal proceedings are structured. | Cannot fully establish the cognitive mechanism through which anchoring operates                                           |
| **Supports Theory**: [[Tversky and Kahneman (1974)]] anchoring and adjustment heuristic with a professional population                                                                                     | **German judicial system**: sentencing norms differ across countries; findings may not generalize to other legal cultures |
| **True Experiment**: Allowed to establish cause-and-effect relationship between the anchor and the sentence.                                                                                               |                                                                                                                           |
| **Pilot Study**: The pilot group helped establish reasonable anchors and served as a control group for the other two conditions, which demonstrated System 1 thinking.                                     |                                                                                                                           |
|                                                                                                                                                                                                            |                                                                                                                           |
