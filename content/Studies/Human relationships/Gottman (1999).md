#PSY #Study #HRelations [[Human Relationships]]
___

**John Gottman** conducted a <u>Prospective Longitudinal Study</u> in his "Love Lab" examining the role of **positive and negative affect** in marital satisfaction and divorce prediction, specifically testing whether the **Active Listening (Validation) Model** of conflict resolution predicts positive outcomes.
___

**Background:** Gottman and Levenson have studied couples in their Love Lab since 1986. Key insight: **69% of relationship problems are "perpetual problems"** rooted in personality differences that never fully resolve. Gottman argues it is *how* couples respond to conflict, not whether conflict exists, that predicts relationship survival.

**Diffuse Physiological Arousal (DPA):** High physiological arousal during conflict reduces the ability to process new information and promotes fight-or-flight responses, actively damaging interaction quality.

**Aim:** To examine which communication patterns predict marital satisfaction and divorce over time, and to test whether active listening / validation during conflict leads to positive outcomes.

---

**Participants:** **124 newlywed couples** recruited via newspaper advertisement. Childless and married for 6 months or less. Sample stratified to reflect Seattle area demographics.

**Method:** <u>Prospective longitudinal study</u> with multi-method data collection.
- Couples completed the **Marital Adjustment Test (MAT)** annually for **6 years**.
- Each couple participated in a **15-minute recorded conflict discussion** about a self-identified relationship problem.
- **Physiological measures** recorded: heart rate, skin conductivity, pulse.
- Two independent coders rated all videos using the **SPAFF (Specific Affect Coding System)**:

| Affect | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Positive** | Interest, validation, affection |
| **Negative (high intensity)** | Contempt, belligerence, defensiveness |
| **Negative (low intensity)** | Whining, sadness, stonewalling |

- Each spouse also rated their own affect by watching the recording back and using a **continuous 9-point dial**.

---

**The Four Horsemen** (destructive communication patterns):

| Horseman | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Criticism** | Attacking partner's character | *"You always..." / "Why are you so..."* |
| **Contempt** | Attacking with insult or mockery; seeing partner as inferior | Sneering, eye-rolling, sarcasm |
| **Defensiveness** | Arguing against partner's concerns; seeing oneself as victim | *"But you also do that / It's not my fault"* |
| **Stonewalling** | Withdrawing to avoid conflict | Silent treatment, leaving, changing subject |

---

**Results:**
- After 6 years, **17 divorces** had occurred.
- **High-intensity negative affect** (belligerence, defensiveness, contempt) predicted divorce in **both spouses**.
- **Low-intensity negative affect** (whining, sadness, stonewalling) predicted divorce in **wives but not husbands**.
- **Surprising finding:** Active listening was rarely demonstrated during conflict and did **not** predict positive outcomes; the Active Listening / Validation Model was **not supported**.
- Despite reviewing 13 years of recordings, Gottman found very few examples of genuine validation in conflict situations.

**Conclusion:** High-intensity negative affect, particularly contempt, is the strongest predictor of relationship breakdown. How couples manage conflict physiologically and emotionally matters far more than whether they employ formal listening techniques.

---

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Data triangulation**: physiological measures, behavioural observation, and self-report enhances validity | **Self-selected sample**: couples who volunteer may differ from the general population |
| **Inter-coder reliability** established using two independent SPAFF coders | SPAFF is standardised but may lack **cross-cultural validity** |
| **Prospective longitudinal design** follows actual divorce outcomes over 6 years | **Reductionist**: focuses solely on communication style, ignoring other variables (finances, health, etc.) |
| Challenges the established **Active Listening Model**; important theoretical contribution | Long-term internal validity is problematic due to extraneous variables accumulating over time |
| High **predictive validity**: communication patterns in year 1 predict outcomes years later | Sample of newlyweds; findings may not apply to couples married for longer periods |

> **Key Concept - The Four Horsemen:**
> *Gottman's Four Horsemen are communication patterns that reliably erode relationship quality. Contempt is the most destructive; it signals that a partner is seen as inferior, fundamentally undermining respect. Critically, Gottman found that active listening during conflict was almost never spontaneously used and did not predict better outcomes, suggesting that relationship health is determined less by conflict resolution techniques and more by the underlying affective tone couples bring to disagreement.*
