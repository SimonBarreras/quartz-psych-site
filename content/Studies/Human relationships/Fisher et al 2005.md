#PSY #Study #HRelations #Biological [[Human Relationships]], [[Evolutionary Explanations - Human Relationships]]
___

**Fisher, Aron and Brown** conducted a <u>Correlational Study using fMRI neuroimaging</u> investigating the **neurobiological basis of romantic love**, identifying which brain regions are activated when individuals view photographs of someone they are intensely in love with, linking love to the brain's **dopaminergic reward and motivation system**.
___

**Aim:** To identify the brain regions associated with the early stage of intense romantic love by comparing neural activation when viewing a photo of a romantic partner versus a neutral acquaintance.

**Participants:** **17 young adults** (10 women, 7 men), mean age **21 years**, who reported being intensely in love. Recruited by **word of mouth and flyers**. Mean reported duration of being in love: **7.4 months**.

**Method:** <u>Correlational study with fMRI neuroimaging</u>.
- Before scanning, participants completed a **semi-structured interview** to establish the duration and intensity of their romantic feelings, followed by the **Passionate Love Scale** (a Likert-scale questionnaire measuring traits associated with romantic love). This allowed comparison of self-reported love intensity with brain activity.
- Inside the fMRI, participants completed a **four-stage repeated procedure** (repeated six times, total duration: 720 seconds / 12 minutes):
  1. **30 seconds**: viewed a photograph of their beloved
  2. **40 seconds**: filler task (counting back from a given number)
  3. **30 seconds**: viewed a photograph of an emotionally neutral acquaintance (control)
  4. **20 seconds**: filler task (counting back from a given number)

**Results:**
- Viewing the partner's photo produced significantly greater activation in:
  - **Ventral tegmental area (VTA)**: a dopamine-rich region central to the brain's reward system
  - **Caudate nucleus**: associated with motivation, goal-directed behaviour, and reward anticipation
- Both regions are part of the **dopaminergic pathway**: a system that generates and transmits dopamine, associated with motivation and feelings of pleasure.
- Love intensity scores correlated positively with activation in these regions: the more passionate the participant, the more active the reward circuitry.
- Activity was notably absent in the amygdala and regions linked to sexual arousal, suggesting love operates as a motivation system rather than a basic emotion.

**Conclusion:** Romantic love activates the brain's **reward and motivation system**, the same dopamine-rich pathways involved in addiction, goal pursuit, and pleasure. According to Fisher, romantic love is not an emotion but a **motivational drive**, a need or craving designed to enable individuals to focus mating energy on one specific partner.

___

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Objective fMRI measures** identify specific brain structures; combined with self-report questionnaires for convergent validity | **Very small sample** (17 participants); self-selected as intensely in love; very limited generalizability |
| **Four-stage procedure with filler tasks** controls for carry-over activation between conditions | **Correlational**: cannot confirm whether brain activation causes love or results from it |
| Links romantic love directly to the **dopaminergic reward pathway**, grounding it biologically | Only captures the **early, intense phase** of love (mean 7.4 months); may not apply to long-term attachment |
| Fisher's conclusion that love is a **motivational drive** is supported by the absence of emotion-region activation | **Reductionist**: reduces a complex human experience to dopamine pathways; ignores cognitive and sociocultural factors |
| Results are consistent with animal research on dopamine and pair bonding | The conclusion that dopamine **causes** love is an inference from correlational fMRI data; blood flow increase is not the same as dopamine release |

> **Key Concept - Love as a Motivational Drive:**
> *Fisher et al. (2005) reframes romantic love not as an emotion but as a powerful motivational drive rooted in the dopaminergic reward system, the same system activated by food, drugs, and primary rewards. The absence of amygdala activation supports this: love does not register as a basic emotion but as a goal-directed craving. This explains the intense preoccupation, focused attention, and obsessive following characteristic of early love, and frames human mating behaviour as evolutionarily continuous with other mammals.*
