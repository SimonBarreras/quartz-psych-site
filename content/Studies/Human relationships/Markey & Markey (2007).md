#Study #HRelations [[Human Relationships]], [[Similarity-Attraction theory]]
___

**Patrick Markey and Charlotte Markey** conducted a <u>Questionnaire Study</u> investigating whether **similarity** in personality drives partner choice in romantic relationships, testing the similarity-attraction model against the "opposites attract" idea.
___

**Aim:** To investigate whether people seek partners who are similar to themselves in personality, and whether similarity or complementarity better predicts relationship harmony.

---

**Participants:**
- **Study 1:** Undergraduate psychology students, each described their **ideal partner** and then **themselves**.
- **Study 2 (main study):** **106 heterosexual couples** (212 participants) recruited via advertisements.

**Method:** <u>Self-report questionnaire</u>.
- Participants described their ideal partner's personality and their own personality using standardised measures.
- Couple similarity and complementarity scores were calculated.
- Relationship quality and harmony were assessed.

---

**Results:**
- **Self-descriptions closely matched ideal partner descriptions**: people seek partners similar to themselves.
- **Similar couples were more harmonious** overall.
- However, **total similarity was not ideal**: the most loving couples showed **some complementarity** in specific traits (e.g., one partner dominant, one submissive).
- This suggests the relationship between similarity and attraction is nuanced: **similarity in most traits, complementarity in dominance-related traits** produces the best outcomes.

**Conclusion:** People are generally attracted to similar others, supporting the **similarity-attraction model**, but some complementarity in specific interpersonal traits (particularly dominance) is associated with greater relationship satisfaction.

---

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Couples design**: both partners measured, reducing single-source bias | **Self-report**: personality ratings are subjective and may reflect idealization |
| **Large sample** (106 couples, 212 participants) in Study 2 enhances reliability | **Sample of young American students/adults**; limits generalisation across cultures and ages |
| Directly tests both similarity **and** complementarity theories | **Correlational**: cannot establish that similarity causes attraction or harmony |
| Nuanced finding (some complementarity is beneficial) adds **ecological validity** | Does not account for how personality similarity changes over the course of a relationship |
| Consistent with a large body of **similarity-attraction research** (e.g., Fiore & Donath, 2005) | Ideal partner descriptions may not reflect actual partner selection behaviour |

> **Key Concept - Similarity-Attraction Model:**
> *The similarity-attraction model proposes that we are drawn to people who are similar to us because similarity validates our worldview and reduces conflict. Markey & Markey (2007) supports this, showing that similar couples are more harmonious, but adds an important nuance: total similarity is not optimal. Some complementarity, particularly in dominance (one partner more assertive, one more accommodating), is associated with the greatest relationship quality.*
