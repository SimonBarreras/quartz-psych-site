#Study #HRelations [[Human Relationships]], [[Bowlby Attachment Theory]]
___

**Cindy Hazan and Phillip Shaver** conducted a <u>Self-Report Questionnaire Study</u>, published as a "love quiz" in a local newspaper, to determine whether there is a correlation between **childhood attachment style** (based on Ainsworth's classification) and **satisfaction and style in adult romantic relationships**.
___

**Background:** Hazan & Shaver argue that romantic love is an **attachment process**, experienced differently because of variations in attachment histories. Bowlby's **continuity hypothesis** proposes that infant attachment styles shape adult relationships through the **internal working model**. Hazan & Shaver translated Ainsworth's infant attachment types into adult equivalents.

**Aim:** To investigate whether childhood attachment style predicts adult romantic relationship patterns, and whether early parental relationships explain adult attachment type.

---

**Participants:**

| Sample | Details |
|---|---|
| **Study 1** | Self-selected, n=620, aged 14-82, mean age 36. 205 males, 415 females; 91% heterosexual; 42% married. |
| **Study 2** | Opportunity sample, n=108 university students, mean age 18, completed as in-class assignment. |

**Method:** <u>Self-report questionnaire</u>.
- Participants chose which of **three attachment style descriptions** best described their feelings in close relationships.
- They described their **most important romantic relationship** (quality, duration, emotional experience).
- They described their **parents' parenting styles** and the quality of their relationship with each parent.

---

**Results:**

| Attachment Style | % (Study 1) | Description of Love | Avg. Relationship Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Secure** | 56% | Happy, friendly, trusting | 10.02 years |
| **Avoidant** | 25% | Fear of intimacy, emotional highs/lows, jealousy | 5.97 years |
| **Anxious-Ambivalent** | 19% | Obsession, desire for reciprocation, emotional extremes | 4.86 years |

- The best predictor of adult attachment type was participants' **perceptions of relationship quality with each parent** and the parents' relationship with each other.
- Correlations were **stronger in Study 2** (student sample), suggesting continuity between childhood and adult attachment experiences may **decrease with age**.

**Conclusion:** There is a correlation between childhood attachment style and adult romantic relationship quality. However, Hazan & Shaver warn against **determinism**: insecurely attached children do not inevitably become insecurely attached adults.

---

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Study 2 (opportunity sample) **strengthens the credibility** of Study 1's findings | **Self-selected sample** (Study 1); may not be representative; more females than males |
| Questionnaires were **anonymous**, reducing social desirability effects | **Self-reported emotions** are unreliable; peak-end rule may distort memories of past relationships |
| Distribution of adult styles **mirrors infant attachment research**; strong convergent validity | Participants described only **one relationship**, which does not establish a pattern across relationships |
| **Large, diverse age range** (14-82) in Study 1 | **Correlational**: cannot establish that childhood attachment causes adult relationship style |
| Influential; supports **Bowlby's continuity hypothesis** empirically | Likert-scale responses subject to cultural differences and response bias |

> **Key Concept - Continuity Hypothesis:**
> *Bowlby argued that early attachment experiences create an internal working model, a cognitive schema of the self, others, and relationships, that persists into adulthood. Hazan & Shaver (1987) provided empirical support, showing that adults' romantic attachment styles mirror childhood patterns. Secure adults form trusting, lasting relationships; avoidant adults fear intimacy; anxious-ambivalent adults experience obsessive, unstable love. Crucially, these are tendencies, not destinies.*
