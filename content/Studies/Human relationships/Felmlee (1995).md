#PSY #Study #HRelations [[Human Relationships]]
___

**Diane Felmlee** conducted a <u>Retrospective Questionnaire Study</u> investigating the phenomenon of **"fatal attractions"** in romantic relationships, the idea that the very quality that initially attracts someone to a partner eventually becomes the primary source of conflict or reason for the relationship ending.
___

**Aim:** To investigate whether the qualities people find most attractive in a partner at the beginning of a relationship are the same qualities that later cause the relationship to end.

---

**Participants:** **301 university students** who had experienced at least one ended romantic relationship.

**Method:** <u>Retrospective self-report questionnaire</u>.
- Participants listed what **attracted them** to their most recent ex-partner.
- They listed what they found **most problematic or irritating** about the same person by the end of the relationship.
- Responses were coded and compared for overlap (same quality appearing as both attraction and complaint).

---

**Results:**
- In **88 out of 301 cases** (approximately 29%), an initially attractive quality became the primary reason for the breakup, a **fatal attraction**.
- Common patterns identified:

| Initial Attraction | Later Problem |
|---|---|
| **Fun to Foolish** | Humorous, fun, carefree -> Immature, avoids problems, uses humour to humiliate |
| **Strong to Domineering** | Confident, strong opinions, leadership -> Uncompromising, authoritarian, potentially abusive |
| **Spontaneous to Unpredictable** | Lives in the moment, exciting -> Lack of planning, unreliable, possibly unfaithful |

- Fatal attractions were more likely when the initial attraction was to an **extreme quality**; the more extreme the trait, the more likely it became problematic.

**Conclusion:** Initially attractive traits frequently become sources of dissatisfaction, suggesting that the same quality that draws people together can drive them apart. This challenges the assumption that attraction is a reliable guide to long-term compatibility.

---

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Large sample** (301 participants) for a qualitative study | **Retrospective self-report**: memories of initial attraction may be distorted after a painful breakup |
| Identifies a **novel, consistent pattern** with high face validity | **University student sample**: may not generalise to older or married populations |
| Findings are **ecologically grounded**, based on real ended relationships | **Predominantly female sample**; limits generalisability across genders |
| Contributes to understanding of both **relationship formation and dissolution** | Coding of open-ended responses introduces potential **researcher subjectivity** |
| Consistent with broader research on how **trait extremity** shapes interpersonal outcomes | Cannot establish whether fatal attractions cause breakups or are a post-hoc rationalisation |

> **Key Concept - Fatal Attraction:**
> *Felmlee's fatal attraction concept reveals a paradox in partner selection: we are often most drawn to qualities that are extreme, and extremity is precisely what makes those same traits problematic over time. Fun becomes foolishness; confidence becomes control. In 88/301 cases, the attraction and the dissolution shared the same root, suggesting that long-term compatibility depends not just on what attracts us, but on whether those qualities remain desirable in moderation.*
