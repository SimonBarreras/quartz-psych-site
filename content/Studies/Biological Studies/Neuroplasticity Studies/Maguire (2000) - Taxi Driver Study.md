#Biological #Study  [[Neuroplasticity]]
___

**Eleanor Maguire et al.** conducted a <u>Quasi-Experiment using MRI scans</u> to investigate whether extensive navigation experience causes **structural changes in the hippocampus** of London taxi drivers, providing some of the most compelling evidence for **neuroplasticity** in the adult human brain.
___

**Aim:** To investigate whether the hippocampi of London taxi drivers differ structurally from non-taxi drivers due to the exceptional navigational demands of their profession, and whether any differences correlate with years of experience.

**Participants:**
- **16 right-handed male London taxi drivers** with at least 1.5 years of experience (mean: 14.3 years)
- **50 healthy right-handed male non-taxi-drivers** as controls, matched for age and handedness

**Method:** <u>Quasi-experiment</u> (IV not manipulated; participants were already taxi drivers or not).
- **MRI scans** were used to measure the **volume and density** of different hippocampal regions.
- A technique called **voxel-based morphometry (VBM)** was used to compare grey matter density.
- The correlation between **years of taxi driving** and hippocampal volume was calculated.
- Taxi drivers in London must pass "The Knowledge", memorizing 25,000 streets, a uniquely demanding spatial memory task.

**Results:**
- Taxi drivers had **significantly greater grey matter volume** in the **posterior hippocampus** compared to controls.
- Controls had relatively greater volume in the **anterior hippocampus**.
- There was a **positive correlation** between the volume of the posterior hippocampus and the number of years spent as a taxi driver; the longer the experience, the larger the posterior hippocampus.
- The **total hippocampal volume** was not significantly different; the change was in the distribution of volume (posterior vs. anterior).

**Conclusion:** The demands of spatial navigation cause **structural changes** in the hippocampus, demonstrating that the adult human brain retains **neuroplasticity**: the ability to reorganize and change in response to experience.

___

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Objective MRI measures** avoid self-report bias | **Quasi-experimental**: cannot confirm navigation caused hippocampal changes (could be pre-existing) |
| **Correlational data** (years of experience) strengthens the environmental explanation | **All-male sample**: findings may not generalize to female taxi drivers or other populations |
| Supports **neuroplasticity** in the adult brain, challenging the idea that the brain stops changing | Small experimental group (16 taxi drivers), limited statistical power |
| Consistent with **Draganski (2004)** and **Rosenzweig et al. (1972)** on experience and brain changes | Cannot rule out that people with larger posterior hippocampi are simply more likely to become taxi drivers |
| Real-world occupational context increases **ecological validity** | The voxel-based morphometry technique has some methodological limitations |

> **Key Concept - Neuroplasticity:**
> *Neuroplasticity is the brain's ability to reorganize itself by forming new neural connections throughout life. Maguire (2000) demonstrated that this occurs at a structural level: years of intensive spatial navigation literally reshapes the hippocampus. This has important implications for education, rehabilitation after brain injury, and our understanding of the limits of human learning.*
