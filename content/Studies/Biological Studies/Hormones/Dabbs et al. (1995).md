#Study #Biological [[Hormones In Human Behaviour]]
___

**James Dabbs et al.** conducted a <u>Quasi-Experiment / Correlational Study</u> investigating the relationship between **testosterone levels** and **violent criminal behaviour**, providing biological evidence for the role of hormones in aggression.
___

**Aim:** To investigate whether higher testosterone levels are associated with more violent crimes and antisocial behaviour in male prison inmates.

**Participants:** **692 adult male prison inmates** in the United States, drawn from a larger prison population.

**Method:** <u>Quasi-experiment / correlational study</u>.
- **Saliva samples** were collected from each inmate to measure testosterone levels non-invasively.
- **Prison records** were reviewed for type of crime committed, disciplinary infractions, and parole board decisions.
- Statistical correlations were calculated between testosterone levels and each behavioural measure.

**Results:**
- Inmates with **higher testosterone levels** were significantly more likely to have committed **violent crimes** (e.g., rape, murder, assault) compared to those with lower levels who had committed non-violent crimes (e.g., theft, drug offences).
- High-testosterone inmates also had **more prison disciplinary violations** and were more likely to be **denied parole**.
- The relationship was statistically significant but the effect size was **modest**, suggesting testosterone is one of many factors.

**Conclusion:** There is a **positive correlation** between testosterone and violent/antisocial behaviour, supporting a biological explanation for aggression. However, the correlation does not establish causation, and social/environmental factors also play a significant role.

___

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Objective biological measure** (saliva samples), less prone to social desirability bias | **Correlational**: cannot establish cause-and-effect. Aggression could raise testosterone, not the reverse |
| **Large sample** (692) increases statistical reliability | **Androcentric**: all-male sample; findings cannot be generalized to women |
| Uses **official prison records**, reducing self-report bias | **Sampling bias**: prison population is not representative of the general population |
| Contributes to **biological explanations of aggression** | Ignores **socialization, culture, and situational factors** that also influence aggression |
| Non-invasive data collection (saliva) is ethically sound | The modest effect size suggests testosterone alone is insufficient as an explanation |

> **Key Concept - Testosterone & Aggression:**
> *Testosterone is an androgen produced primarily in the testes. It has been linked to dominance-seeking and aggressive behaviour. However, the relationship is bidirectional: testosterone can increase aggression, but aggressive situations and victories can also raise testosterone levels (the 'challenge hypothesis').*
