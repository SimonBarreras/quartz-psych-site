#Study #Biological [[Genetics And Human Behaviour]]
___

**Caspi et al. (2003)** conducted a <u>Correlational Study</u> investigating the role of gene mutation (particularly the 5-HTT serotonin transporter gene) in the development of major depressive disorder (MDD). 
___
**Aim:** To investigate the evidence for a gene-environment interaction for a mutation of the serotonin transporter gene (5-HTT) (involved in the re-uptake of serotonin in brain synapses)

**Participants:** **847 New Zealand 26-year-olds** from the Dunedin Longitudinal Study, assessed for mental health on an **every-other-year basis until age 21**. Participants were grouped by genotype:
	- **Group 1**: two short alleles (s/s)
	- **Group 2**: one short, one long allele (s/l)
	- **Group 3**: two long alleles (l/l)

Roughly **43% of people** carry shorter alleles. 

**Method:** <u>correlational study</u>, 
- **Genetic analysis:** DNA samples taken to determine 5-HTT genotype.
- **Stressful life events questionnaire:** Participants self-reported frequency of 14 different stressful events between ages 21-26 (financial, employment, health and relationship stressors).
- **Depression:** Assessed via structured psychiatric interviews. 

**Results:**
- Participants with 1+ short alleles demonstrated more symptoms of depression and suicidal ideation in response to stressful life events. 
- The effect was stronger for those with 3+ stressful life events. Interaction of the genes with stressful life events increased one's likelihood of developing depression. 
- Those with **long alleles (l/l)** were more **resilient** to depression even under high stress.
- Genotype alone (with no stressors) showed **no significant difference** in depression rates, confirming the interaction effect.

> In a later study by Wilhelm et al (2006), the researcher looked at DNA samples from 127 people who were part of a longitudinal (25y - 5 year interval recordings of major life events and signs of depression) prospective study looking at mental health. 80% of those with **s/s** alleles became depressed after 3+ more negative life events in a year, while only 30% of those with **l/l** alleles developed illness in similar situations. 
> - The study also found that childhood maltreatment predicted adult depression only among individuals carrying a short allele. 

**Conclusion:** The 5-HTT gene does not cause depression directly, but moderates stress sensitivity, supporting the **diathesis-stress model**: genetic vulnerability + environmental stress = increased risk.

## Evaluation

| Strengths                                                          | Limitations                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Supporting Research and evidence** (e.g., Wilhelm et al)         | Stressful life events were **self-reported**, so subjective and prone to recall bias. It may be the **salience** of life events which plays a role in depression. |
| **Large sample** from the Dunedin study increases reliability.     | Sample is largely white New Zealanders, limiting **cultural generalizability**.<br>also is a sample used to being studied from longitudinal study.                |
| Objective **biological measures** (DNA) increase internal validity | Assumption that serotonin causes depression                                                                                                                       |
| Supports the **diathesis-stress model** of depression              | Depression is **polygenic**, so we cannot isolate the 5-HTT gene as the sole factor. + some participants became depressed without carrying the **s/s** alleles    |
| **Methodological triangulation** (genetic testing, questionnaire)  | Ethical concerns around DNA use without full initial disclosure                                                                                                   |
|                                                                    | Failure of replication of results in later studies e.g., Risch et al (2009) ∴ low reliability                                                                     |

> **Key Concept - Diathesis-Stress Model:**
> *The diathesis-stress model proposes that a genetic predisposition (diathesis) alone is not sufficient to cause a disorder. It is the combination of genetic vulnerability and environmental stressors that triggers expression of the disorder. Caspi et al. (2003) demonstrates this directly: the 5-HTT short allele increased sensitivity to stress, but participants with no stressful life events showed no significant difference in depression rates regardless of genotype. The gene moderates the response to stress; it does not cause depression on its own.*
