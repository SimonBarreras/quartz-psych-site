#Study #Biological [[Genetics And Human Behaviour]]
___

**Thomas Bouchard et al.** conducted a <u>Natural Experiment using Twin Study methodology</u> investigating the relative contributions of **genetics and environment** to personality, intelligence, and behaviour, by comparing identical twins raised apart from birth with those raised together.
___

**Aim:** To investigate the extent to which psychological characteristics (intelligence, personality, attitudes, and interests) are determined by genetic factors, by studying monozygotic (MZ) twins who were separated shortly after birth and raised in different environments.

**Participants:** **137 pairs of twins** recruited over 20 years as part of the **Minnesota Study of Twins Reared Apart (MISTRA)**:
- **MZ twins reared apart (MZA)**: Genetically identical, raised in different families and environments
- **MZ twins reared together (MZT)**: Genetically identical, raised together (comparison group)
- **DZ twins reared apart (DZA)**: Fraternal (50% shared genes), raised apart

Participants were recruited through media coverage and self-referral from across the world.

**Method:** <u>Natural experiment</u> using twin study methodology.
- Twin pairs were brought to the University of Minnesota for approximately **50 hours of medical and psychological assessment** over one week.
- Assessments included:
  - **IQ tests** (multiple standardized measures)
  - **Personality inventories** (e.g., Multidimensional Personality Questionnaire)
  - Physiological measures (heart rate, brain wave patterns, reaction time)
  - Interviews on life history, interests, values, and social attitudes
- **Concordance rates** and **heritability estimates** were calculated for each trait.

**Results:**
- MZ twins reared apart showed **strikingly similar** scores on IQ, personality, and interests, despite being raised in different environments.
- **IQ heritability estimate: approximately 70%**, meaning roughly 70% of variance in IQ scores was attributable to genetic factors.
- **Personality heritability estimates: approximately 50%** for most traits (e.g., extraversion, neuroticism, conscientiousness).
- MZA twins were nearly as similar as MZT twins on most measures, suggesting the shared environment of growing up together contributes relatively little beyond genetic similarity.
- Some pairs showed remarkable behavioural similarities (e.g., same hobbies, mannerisms, and preferences) despite having never met.

**Conclusion:** Genetic factors account for a substantial proportion of variance in intelligence, personality, and many behaviours. The similarity between MZA twins, who share only genes and not environment, provides strong evidence for the role of heredity in shaping human behaviour.

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **MZA design is the most powerful** test of genetic influence: removes shared environment as a confound | **Self-selected sample**: twins volunteered after media coverage; may not be representative |
| **Large, diverse sample** (137 pairs, recruited internationally) increases reliability | **Selective placement**: adoption agencies sometimes matched twins to similar families, confounding the environment/genes separation |
| **Multiple standardized measures** across a week of assessment; high methodological rigour | MZ twins share a **prenatal environment**; prenatal influences may account for some similarity |
| Heritability estimates are consistent with other twin and adoption studies | **Heritability is population-specific**: estimates apply to the sampled population and environment, not universally |
| Foundational for **behavioural genetics**; directly informs the nature-nurture debate | Does not identify which specific genes are responsible; heritability does not mean genetic determinism |

> **Key Concept - Heritability:**
> *Heritability refers to the proportion of variance in a trait within a population that is attributable to genetic differences. Bouchard et al. (1990) found heritability estimates of around 70% for IQ and 50% for personality. Critically, heritability does not mean a trait is fixed or unchangeable; it means that, within the studied population and environment, genetic differences account for more of the variation than environmental differences do.*
