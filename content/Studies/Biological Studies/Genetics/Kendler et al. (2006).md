#Study #Biological [[Genetics And Human Behaviour]]
___

**Kendler et al.** conducted a <u>Large-Scale Twin Study</u> investigating the heritability of **major depressive disorder (MDD)**, gender differences in that heritability, and whether genetic and environmental factors differ across generations.

___

**Aim:** To investigate three questions:
1. Whether the previously estimated 35-45% heritability of major depression holds in a large Swedish sample.
2. Whether there are significant gender differences in heritability of major depression.
3. Whether genetic and environmental factors differ across time (birth cohorts).

**Participants:** **15,493 complete twin pairs** from the **Swedish Twin Registry**. Only twins whose zygosity could be verified were included. Included male-male MZ/DZ, female-female MZ/DZ, and male-female DZ pairs.

**Method:** <u>Twin study</u> using telephone interviews.
- A team of trained interviewers conducted telephone interviews between **March 1998 and January 2003**.
- Lifetime major depression was assessed using **modified DSM-IV criteria**.
- **8,056 twins** met criteria for a diagnosis of major depression at some point in their lives; **322** voluntarily reported antidepressant treatment history.
- Interviewers also measured **shared environment** (years living together) and **individual-specific environment** (adult personal life events).

**Results:**

| Sex and Zygosity | Twin Pairs | Concordance |
|---|---|---|
| Female-Female MZ | 2,317 | **0.44** |
| Female-Female DZ | 3,185 | **0.16** |
| Male-Male MZ | 1,774 | **0.31** |
| Male-Male DZ | 2,584 | **0.11** |
| Male-Female DZ | 5,633 | **0.11** |

- MZ concordance was significantly higher than DZ in both sexes, confirming a genetic contribution.
- Concordance rates were **significantly higher in women** than men, suggesting heritability is greater for females.
- **Heritability estimate: 0.38** (38%), consistent with prior research.
- **No correlation** was found between the number of years twins lived together and lifetime major depression, suggesting the shared family environment contributes little.
- No significant differences in the role of genetic or environmental factors across three birth cohorts (spanning 1900-1958), including pre and post-World War II.
- Some genetic risk factors for major depression appear to be **sex-specific**.

**Conclusion:** Major depression has a moderate but consistent heritability (~38%), which is higher in women than men. The individual-specific environment (unique personal life events) contributes more to depression risk than the shared environment. Genetic risk factors are partially sex-specific.

---

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Very large sample** (15,493 twin pairs): one of the largest twin studies on depression ever conducted | **Correlational**: cannot establish cause-and-effect; no specific genes were identified or tested |
| Results are **consistent with prior research** (35-45% heritability), strengthening reliability | **Self-reported** life events and depression history: men may under-report depression, inflating apparent gender differences |
| **Verified zygosity**: only twins with confirmed MZ/DZ status were included, increasing internal validity | Diagnosis was not made directly by interviewers: they accepted clinical diagnoses reported by participants, which may be unreliable; telephone diagnosis is of questionable validity |
| Directly addresses **gender differences** in heritability, an underexplored area | **Twin registry sample**: volunteers may not represent the general population |
| Consistent with **diathesis-stress model**: individual-specific environment (stressful life events) matters more than shared environment | Cannot determine which specific genes are involved in the 38% heritable component |

> **Key Concept - MZ vs DZ Concordance Rates:**
> *If depression were entirely genetic, MZ concordance would be 100%. If entirely environmental, MZ and DZ rates would be equal. Kendler et al. (2006) found female MZ concordance (0.44) significantly higher than female DZ (0.16), giving a heritability estimate of 0.38. The fact that concordance is far from 100% even in genetically identical twins confirms that environment, specifically individual life experiences rather than shared family environment, plays the larger role in whether the genetic predisposition is expressed.*
