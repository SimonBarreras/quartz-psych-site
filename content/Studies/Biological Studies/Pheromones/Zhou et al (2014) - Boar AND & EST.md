#PSY #Study #Biological #Pheromones [[Evolutionary Explanations - Human Relationships]], [[Human Relationships]]
___

**Zhou et al.** conducted a <u>True Lab Experiment</u> to test whether the human chemosignals **androstadienone (AND)** and **estratetraenol (EST)** influence the **perception of biological sex** in a manner consistent with their functioning as human pheromones.
___

**Background:**
- **Androstadienone (AND)** is a human steroid found in male semen and sweat. It heightens sympathetic arousal, alters cortisol, and promotes positive mood in females. It activates the hypothalamus in heterosexual females and homosexual males, but **not** in heterosexual males or homosexual females.
- **Estratetraenol (EST)** is the female equivalent found in urine.
- A key challenge: humans lack a **functional vomeronasal organ (VNO)**, the receptor used by most animals to detect pheromones, making human pheromone research contentious.

**Aim:** To investigate whether AND and EST function as sex-specific human pheromones by biasing the perception of biological sex in ways consistent with each participant's sexual orientation.

---

**Participants:** **96 healthy non-smokers** in four groups:

| Group | n |
|---|---|
| Heterosexual males | 24 |
| Heterosexual females | 24 |
| Homosexual males | 24 |
| Bisexual/homosexual females | 24 |

**Method:** <u>True experiment</u> with manipulated IV, allowing cause-and-effect conclusions.
- A **point-light walker (PLW) task** was used: moving dots representing human motion with deliberately ambiguous biological sex.
- Participants identified the sex of the figure while exposed to one of three scents applied under the nose:
  - AND mixed with cloves
  - EST mixed with cloves
  - Clove oil only (control)
- Completed on **three consecutive days**, **counterbalanced** to control for order effects.

---

**Results:**

| Chemosignal | Effect on heterosexual females | Effect on homosexual males | Effect on heterosexual males | Effect on bisexual/lesbian females |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AND** | Perceived walkers as more masculine | Perceived walkers as more masculine | No significant effect | No significant effect |
| **EST** | No significant effect | No significant effect | Perceived walkers as more feminine | Effect not statistically significant |

- Effects aligned with **who the participant is typically attracted to**, not their own biological sex.
- Control odour produced no consistent directional bias.

**Conclusion:** AND and EST affect gender perception in a **sexuality-specific manner** consistent with a pheromone-like function, supporting the existence of human chemosignals that influence social perception.

---

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **True experiment** with manipulated IV; allows cause-and-effect conclusions | **Hare et al. (2017)** failed to replicate the findings, questioning reliability |
| **Counterbalanced design** controls for order effects | Doses of AND and EST used were **significantly higher** than natural human secretion levels, limiting ecological validity |
| Results across different gender/sexuality groups strengthen the **biological interpretation** | Effects were on **gender perception**, not actual attraction or mate choice |
| Directly tests the existence of **human pheromones** in a controlled way | Humans lack a functional VNO; the mechanism by which AND/EST are detected remains unclear |
| Inclusion of **four sexual orientation groups** is a major methodological strength | Sample consisted of healthy young non-smokers; generalisability is limited |

> **Key Concept - Human Pheromones & the VNO Problem:**
> *Most animals detect pheromones via the vomeronasal organ (VNO). Humans appear to lack a functional VNO, making the existence of human pheromones controversial. Zhou et al. (2014) sidesteps this by measuring behavioural effects of AND and EST, showing that these chemosignals systematically bias gender perception in a sexuality-specific way. However, failed replications (Hare et al., 2017) and the use of supra-natural doses mean the evidence remains contested.*
