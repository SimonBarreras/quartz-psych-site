#Study #HRelations #Biological #Pheromones [[Evolutionary Explanations - Human Relationships]], [[Human Relationships]]
___
**Wedekind** conducted a <u>Double-Blind Lab Experiment</u>, known as the **"Smelly T-Shirt Study"**, to determine whether women prefer the body odour of men with **dissimilar Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) genes**, testing an evolutionary prediction about immune-based mate selection.
___

**Background:**
- The **Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC)** is a group of genes that enable the immune system to recognize pathogens.
- MHC genes are **co-dominant**: offspring inherit and express *both* parental sets.
- More diverse parental MHC leads to a **stronger offspring immune system**.
- Evolutionary logic therefore predicts a mechanism for detecting MHC dissimilarity in potential mates.

**Aim:** To investigate whether women prefer the body odour of MHC-dissimilar men as an evolutionary mechanism to maximize offspring immune diversity.

---

**Participants:** **49 female** and **44 male** students from the **University of Bern, Switzerland**.
- MHC genotyped with wide variance included.
- Students drawn from different courses (women: biology/psychology; men: chemistry/physics/geography), making them unlikely to know each other.
- Whether women were taking **oral contraceptives** was recorded.

**Method:** <u>Double-blind experiment</u>.
- Men wore T-shirts for **two nights** and kept them in an **open plastic bag during the day**. Controlled conditions: perfume-free soap, no deodorant, no perfume, no alcohol, no spicy food, no tobacco, no sexual activity.
- Women used a **nose spray for 14 days before the experiment** to support nasal mucous membrane regeneration and as a preventive measure against colds.
- Women were tested in the **second week after the beginning of menstruation** (peak olfactory sensitivity).
- Women also read Süskind's *Perfume* beforehand to heighten smell awareness.
- Each woman rated **7 T-shirt odours** (presented in cardboard boxes with a smelling hole) for **intensity** (0-10) and **pleasantness/sexiness** (0-10, with 5 as neutral):
  - 3 T-shirts from **MHC-similar** men
  - 3 T-shirts from **MHC-dissimilar** men
  - 1 **unworn control** T-shirt

---

**Results:**
- Women rated MHC-dissimilar male odours as **significantly more pleasant** than MHC-similar ones.
- This preference was **reversed** in women taking **oral contraceptives**, suggesting the pill disrupts natural MHC-based mate preference (mimicking pregnancy, where proximity to similar kin may be adaptive).

**Conclusion:** Women show a preference for MHC-dissimilar male body odour, consistent with an evolved mechanism for selecting genetically compatible mates to **maximise offspring immune diversity**.

---

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Double-blind design** minimises demand characteristics and experimenter bias | **Small, homogeneous sample** (young Swiss university students); limited generalisability |
| **Successfully replicated** by Jacob et al. (2002) | **Reductionist**: ignores cognitive and sociocultural influences on mate selection |
| Findings **mirror animal research**: female mice also prefer MHC-dissimilar males (Yamazaki et al., 1976) | Women on oral contraceptives were included in ratings; contraceptive status may have confounded some comparisons |
| Supported by **clinical research**: couples with repeated miscarriages share more MHC than control couples (Beer et al., 1985) | Odour ratings are subjective; individual differences in smell sensitivity not controlled |
| **Ethical standards met**: informed consent and full debriefing provided | Real-world attraction involves many more cues than scent alone |

> **Key Concept - MHC & The Contraceptive Reversal:**
> *The contraceptive reversal finding in Wedekind (1995) is particularly significant. Oral contraceptives mimic the hormonal state of pregnancy, during which proximity to genetically similar kin (rather than dissimilar mates) may be evolutionarily advantageous. This accidental reversal of a natural preference suggests that the modern use of hormonal contraceptives may subtly interfere with evolved mate selection mechanisms, with potential implications for relationship satisfaction and fertility.*
