**Martinez and Kesner** conducted a <u>Lab Experiment on rats</u> investigating the causal role of **acetylcholine (ACh)** in **spatial memory**, using pharmacological manipulation to either increase or decrease ACh activity and measuring the effect on maze performance.
___

**Aim:** To investigate whether acetylcholine levels in the brain have a causal effect on memory performance in rats navigating a spatial maze.

**Participants:** Laboratory rats, randomly assigned to three groups.

**Method:** <u>Lab experiment</u> with independent groups design.

| Group | Drug | Effect on ACh |
|---|---|---|
| **Group 1 - Scopolamine** | Muscarinic ACh receptor antagonist | Reduces ACh activity by blocking receptors |
| **Group 2 - Physostigmine** | Acetylcholinesterase inhibitor (blocks the enzyme that breaks down ACh) | Increases ACh activity |
| **Group 3 - Control** | Saline injection (no drug) | No change |

- All rats were trained on a **spatial maze** and then tested following drug injection.
- **Dependent variable**: Number of errors made and time taken to complete the maze.

**Results:**

| Group | Performance |
|---|---|
| **Scopolamine (reduced ACh)** | More errors, slower; significantly worse than control |
| **Physostigmine (increased ACh)** | Fewer errors, faster; significantly better than control |
| **Control** | Baseline performance |

**Conclusion:** ACh levels have a **direct causal effect** on spatial memory performance. Reducing ACh impairs memory; increasing ACh enhances it. This confirms acetylcholine's critical role in the encoding and retrieval of spatial memories, likely mediated through the hippocampus.

___

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Controlled lab experiment**: high internal validity; random assignment isolates ACh as the variable | **Animal research**: rats' neurochemistry is similar to humans, but direct generalization is not guaranteed |
| **Three-group design** (reduce, increase, control) provides a strong comparative framework | **Ethical concerns**: invasive injections; animals were used as subjects |
| Results are **consistent and clear**; both directions of ACh manipulation confirmed the hypothesis | Drugs may affect **other neurotransmitter systems** beyond ACh alone |
| Consistent with and complemented by **Antonova et al. (2011)** in human participants | Artificial maze task may not reflect the full complexity of real-world memory |
| Helped establish **acetylcholine as key to memory**; informs Alzheimer's research | Scopolamine and physostigmine affect ACh systemically, not only in the hippocampus |

> **Key Concept - Acetylcholinesterase Inhibitors:**
> *Physostigmine works by blocking acetylcholinesterase, the enzyme that breaks down ACh in the synapse. This prolongs ACh's presence and enhances its effects. The same principle underlies drugs used to treat Alzheimer's disease (e.g., donepezil), making Martinez & Kesner directly clinically relevant.*
