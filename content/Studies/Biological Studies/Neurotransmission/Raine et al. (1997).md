#Study #Biological [[Neurotransmission]], [[Localization of function]]
___

**Adrian Raine et al.** conducted a <u>Quasi-Experiment using PET scans</u> to investigate whether **murderers pleading not guilty by reason of insanity (NGRI)** show different patterns of **brain activity** compared to non-murderers, providing biological evidence for the neural basis of violent behaviour.
___

**Aim:** To investigate whether murderers who pleaded NGRI show significantly different brain glucose metabolism (brain activity) compared to matched non-murderer controls, using PET scans.

**Participants:**
- **41 murderers** (39 male, 2 female) who had pleaded NGRI, referred by courts or lawyers
- **41 matched controls** (non-murderers), matched for age and sex; 6 were matched for schizophrenia diagnosis

**Method:** <u>Quasi-experiment</u> (IV was not manipulated; participants were already murderers or not).
- Participants performed a **continuous performance task** (CPT) to activate the brain.
- A **PET scan** (positron emission tomography) measured glucose metabolism across 14 brain regions.
- Brain activity levels were compared between the two groups.

**Results:**
- Murderers showed **reduced activity** in the **prefrontal cortex** (linked to impulse control, planning, and emotional regulation).
- Reduced activity was also found in the **corpus callosum**, **amygdala**, and **hippocampus**.
- **Increased activity** was found in the **thalamus** and **caudate** in the left hemisphere.
- These differences suggest abnormal functioning in brain areas associated with aggression regulation and emotional processing.

**Conclusion:** Murderers who plead NGRI show distinct patterns of brain activity, particularly in the prefrontal cortex, supporting a **biological basis for violent behaviour**. However, Raine cautioned that this does not mean biology determines crime, only that it is a contributing factor.

___

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Objective biological measure** (PET scan) avoids self-report bias | **Quasi-experimental**: cannot establish direct cause-and-effect |
| **Matched controls** reduce confounding variables | Sample is NGRI murderers, not representative of all violent offenders |
| Contributes to **localization of function** research (prefrontal cortex) | Reduced PFC activity may be a result of violence, not a cause |
| Supports biological explanations for **aggression and antisocial behaviour** | **Ethical concerns**: using brain scans as legal evidence raises issues of biological determinism and criminal justice implications |
| Replicable standardized procedure | Small, specific sample limits **generalizability** |
