#PSY #SIT  Proposed by [[Tajfel (1971) - Klee or Kandinsky]] [[Minimal Group Paradigm]]
___
>[!Quote]
>*Humans have a basic need to belong*

>[!Note]
>Social Identity Theory argues that a person has not just one "*personal self*", but rather *several social selves* that correspond to a **group membership**. Humans have a need to know their role/value in social contexts. Hence, tend to categorize ourselves in terms of group membership. 
>
>Theory predicts that when one of our **Social identities** becomes **salient**; it will influence our behaviour

- Group memberships give rise to social identities and comparison with other groups, this might lead to bias in information processing like stereotyping and discrimination

Group Membership can be divided into three parts:

1. Social categorization (in-groups and out-groups)
2. Social identification ("I'm part of this or that" positive effect in self esteem)
3. Social comparison (Positive distinctive ness)
___
# **Social Identity theory has 4 stages:**

1. Humans seek **Positive social identities**
2. Self & Social **Categorization**
3. **Social Comparison**
4. Humans seek for **Positive distinctiveness** (In-group and Out-group) (maximize difference / discrimination)
	 with tendency to a concept of **in-group superiority or preference.**


SIT can predict certain behaviour, such as 
- Ethnocentrism
- In-group serving bias

|           | Postitive Attributtion | Negative Attribution |
| --------- | ---------------------- | -------------------- |
| In-group  | Dispositional          | Situational          |
| Out-group | Situational            | Dispositional        |
- In-group favoritism
- Intergroup differentiation
	- Generalization and use of stereotypes
	- Creation of in-group norms & values
	- Conformity to established norms & values
