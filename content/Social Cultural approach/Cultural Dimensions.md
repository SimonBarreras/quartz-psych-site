#PSY #Sociocultural [[Hofstede (2001)]], [[Berry (1967)]]
___

**Cultural dimensions** are spectrums of behaviour used to represent the norms and values of different cultures. Rather than describing individual behaviour, dimensions describe the general trends of a given culture and can be used to compare cultures with each other.

Hofstede's framework is the most widely used model of cultural dimensions in IB Psychology. His research asked employees of the multinational company **IBM** to fill in surveys about workplace morale and values. He then carried out a **factor analysis** on the responses, focusing on key differences submitted by employees in different countries. This is an example of an **etic approach**: applying a standardized framework from the outside to compare cultures.

His original study covered the **40 most represented countries** in the surveys. Hofstede (2001) later extended this with 30 years of data, claiming results stayed largely consistent.

---

## The Six Cultural Dimensions

| Dimension | Description |
|---|---|
| **Individualism vs. Collectivism** | The degree to which people are integrated into groups. "I" vs. "we" orientation. In individualist cultures, everyone is expected to look after themselves and their immediate family. In collectivist cultures, people are integrated into strong, cohesive in-groups that provide support and protection. |
| **Power Distance Index (PDI)** | The extent to which a culture respects authority and status; the degree of acceptance of unequal distribution of power. High PDI cultures expect clear hierarchies; low PDI cultures prefer consensus. |
| **Uncertainty Avoidance Index (UAI)** | A society's tolerance for ambiguity. High UAI cultures prefer strict rules and resist change. Low UAI cultures are more open to ambiguity and new ideas. |
| **Masculinity vs. Femininity** | Masculine societies are defined by a focus on achievement, competition, and wealth. Feminine societies focus on cooperation, relationships, and quality of life. |
| **Long-Term vs. Short-Term Orientation** | The connection to the past and attitude toward the future. Short-term orientation values tradition and immediate reward. Long-term orientation focuses on perseverance and future benefit. |
| **Indulgence vs. Restraint** | Indulgent cultures allow people to enjoy life freely; restrained cultures suppress gratification through strict social norms. Indulgent cultures tend to believe they are in control of their lives; restrained cultures are more fatalistic. |

> **For the exam:** You only need to know **one** cultural dimension in depth. The IB focuses most heavily on **individualism vs. collectivism**, as it is the most widely researched and directly supported by multiple studies.

## Individualism vs. Collectivism in Detail

| Individualist Cultures | Collectivist Cultures |
|---|---|
| Uniqueness is valued | Social harmony is valued |
| Speaking one's mind is important | Modesty is important |
| Self defined by individual achievement | Self defined by group membership |
| Freedom and autonomy are valued | Common fate/history guides decision-making |
| Rule-breaking leads to guilt and loss of self-respect | Rule-breaking leads to shame and loss of face |
| Self-reliance is seen as a virtue | Shared responsibility and interdependence are a way of life |

Markus and Kitayama (1991) captured this distinction with two proverbs: *"In America, the squeaky wheel gets the grease; in Japan, the nail that stands out gets pounded down."* They argue that perceiving a boundary between the individual and the social environment is distinctly Western; non-Western cultures tend toward connectedness.

## How Individualism vs. Collectivism Affects Behaviour

**Conformity** (Berry, 1967): Berry used an adapted Asch paradigm to compare conformity across three cultures. The **Temne of Sierra Leone** (collectivist, rice farming) showed significantly higher conformity when told what other Temne believed, even when it was incorrect. The **Inuit of Baffin Island** (individualist, hunting) showed lower conformity than even the Scottish reference group. Importantly, there was **no significant difference within groups** based on degree of Western exposure, suggesting that cultural identity itself, not degree of Westernisation, drives the effect.

**Memory** (Kulkofsky et al.): Social sharing of memories is stronger in collectivist cultures, where communal storytelling is a social norm. This produces stronger flashbulb memories for culturally significant events.

## Evaluation of Cultural Dimensions Theory

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| **Enormous sample** (116,000+ IBM employees); scores consistent across 30 years of data | **IBM sample**: corporate, educated employees are not representative of their national populations |
| **Factor analysis** with team-based coding; good inter-rater reliability between researchers | **Researcher bias** in factor analysis: researchers decide which correlations are significant; risk of confirmation bias |
| Provides a **standardized, comparable framework** across cultures; widely used in psychology, business, and diplomacy | Hofstede studied **work environments only**; attitudes toward work may not transfer to all areas of daily life |
| **Etic approach** allows direct cross-cultural comparison | **Etic limitation**: dimensions are defined from a Western framework and may not capture concepts important to non-Western cultures |
| Consistent with Berry (1967), Kulkofsky et al., and conformity research | **Correlational**: cannot argue that culture *causes* these behaviours |
| Hofstede warns against the **ecological fallacy** himself | Reduces complex cultural identities to numerical scores, risking stereotyping and stereotype threat |

> **Ecological fallacy (Hofstede's own warning):** When comparing two cultures, it cannot be assumed that two individuals from those cultures must differ from each other, or that any one member of a culture will always demonstrate the cultural norm. Dimensions describe trends across populations, not universal rules for individuals.

## IB Exam Application

**Possible essay question (from your InThinking source):**
*"Discuss the role of one cultural dimension on human behaviour."*

**Approach:**
- Define the dimension (individualism vs. collectivism)
- Explain how it influences behaviour (conformity, memory sharing)
- Use Berry (1967) and Kulkofsky as supporting studies
- Evaluate the research (IBM sample, ecological fallacy, factor analysis bias, etic limitations)
- Think globally: what are the difficulties of measuring culture? Can variables be operationalized? What assumptions must we accept?
