## Section A: Short Answer Questions (SAQs) 27 marks 

>[!warning] Key Rule No critical thinking or evaluation is required in Section A. Focus entirely on **knowledge, understanding, and application** (APPR structure).
### SAQ Structure

- **Describe** — give a detailed account of a theory, concept, or study
- **Explain** — give a clear account of causes, reasons, or mechanisms
- **Outline** — give a brief account; less detail than 'describe'

- 3 compulsory SAQs (9 marks per SAQ)
> [!tip] APPR structure 
> **A — Aim:** State what you are going to address and briefly define the concept.
> 
> **P — Point:** Describe the relevant theory or concept clearly, using psychological terminology.
> 
> **P — Proof:** Describe a relevant study (aim, procedure, findings, conclusion). Name researcher(s) and year.
> 
> **R — Relevance:** Explicitly link the study back to the question/command term.

- Aim for **200–350 words** per SAQ
- Spend approximately **20 minutes** per SAQ
- No introduction or conclusion needed — be direct and focused
- Always name at least one researcher and year

### SAQ Markbands (0–9)

| Marks | What examiners look for                                                                                           |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 7–9   | Accurate, relevant knowledge; terminology used correctly; study well described; clear link to question throughout |
| 4–6   | Mostly relevant; some accurate knowledge but incomplete or partially linked                                       |
| 1–3   | Vague or inaccurate knowledge; limited relevance; study absent or barely described                                |

## Section B: Extended Response Questions (ERQs) 22 marks

3 prompts provided (one per approach). Choose and answer **1**. Worth **22 marks**.
> [!warning] Key Rule Full essay structure is required. Critical thinking is **essential** and heavily rewarded. Suggested time: **60 minutes**. Aim for **700–900 words**

### ERQ Essay Structure

> [!tip] Essay Structure 
> **1. Introduction** — Define key terms. State your thesis/argument clearly. Signpost what the essay will cover.
> 
> **2. Study 1** — Describe aim, participants, procedure, findings, and conclusion. Link explicitly to the question.
> 
> **3. Specific Critical Thinking (CT)** — Evaluate Study 1 specifically: methodological strengths/limitations, ethical considerations, sample issues.
> 
> **4. Study 2** — Second study, similarly described and linked to the essay question.
> 
> **5. Holistic Critical Thinking (CT)** — Evaluate the broader body of evidence: conflicting studies, cultural bias, ethical limitations of the field, generalisability.
> 
> **6. Evaluation** — Assess the overall value of the theories/studies in answering the question. Strengths and limitations of the approach.
> 
> **7. Conclusion** — Directly answer the question. Summarise the weight of evidence. Return to your thesis.

- Critical thinking must be substantive, vague comments like _'the study lacked ecological validity'_ without explanation will not earn marks
- Every paragraph should visibly link back to the question,  do not drift into irrelevant knowledge
#### ERQ Command Terms

> [!note] Command Terms 
> **Discuss** — Present evidence and arguments for and against; weigh them up and reach a balanced conclusion. Most common command term.
> 
> **Evaluate** — Make a judgement about the value or limitations of something using evidence. Requires a supported conclusion.
> 
> **Contrast** — Explicitly point out differences between two or more things. Do not describe similarities.
> 
> **To what extent** — Weigh up evidence and argue a clear position. Requires a definitive conclusion.

#### ERQ Markbands

|Marks|Knowledge & Understanding|Critical Thinking / Evaluation|
|---|---|---|
|7–9|Clear, focused answer. Accurate concepts with relevant terminology. Study well-described and linked to the question.|Thorough evaluation of method, ethics, or application. Limitations clearly identified and explained.|
|4–6|Answer is mostly relevant. Some accurate knowledge but may be incomplete or partially linked.|Some evaluation present but may be superficial or not fully developed.|
|1–3|Limited relevance to the question. Knowledge is vague or inaccurate.|Little or no evaluation. May just describe without critical analysis.|

#### Critical Thinking:

##### Specific CT — evaluating an individual study

- **Sample bias**: Was the sample representative? (WEIRD: Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, Democratic)
- **Methodology**: Lab experiment vs field study? Controlled but low ecological validity vs ecologically valid but less controlled?
- **Replication**: Has the study been replicated? Does it have high test-retest reliability?
- **Ethics**: Deception, lack of informed consent, psychological harm, right to withdraw?
- **Demand characteristics**: Did participants guess the study's aim and change their behaviour?

##### Holistic CT — evaluating the body of evidence

- **Cultural bias**: Do findings apply universally, or are they culture-specific? (e.g. collectivist vs individualist societies)
- **Gender bias**: Were male/female samples equivalent? Are findings generalised inappropriately?
- **Conflicting evidence**: Are there studies that contradict the claim? What does the balance of evidence suggest?
- **Ethical limitations**: Has the field been constrained by ethical guidelines, limiting what can be studied?
- **Reductionism**: Does the explanation oversimplify complex behaviour by reducing it to one factor?
