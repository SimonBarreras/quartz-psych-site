## The Three Human Relationships Topics

The 3 questions will each come from a different subtopic. The three subtopics are:

1. **Personal relationships** (formation, maintenance, dissolution)
2. **Group dynamics** (origins of conflict, conflict resolution, cooperation, prosocial behaviour)
3. **Social responsibility** (bystanderism, promoting prosocial behaviour, prejudice and discrimination)

> [!tip] Strategy note
> Since there is one question per subtopic, if you have covered all three you can choose whichever question suits your strongest studies. Most students prepare one strong study pairing per subtopic.

## ERQ Format and Mark Scheme

Each question is worth **22 marks** across three criteria:

| Criterion | Marks | What it assesses |
|---|---|---|
| A: Focus | 2 marks | Does the answer actually address the question? |
| B: Knowledge and Understanding | 10 marks | Accuracy of psychological knowledge, use of terminology, quality of study description |
| C: Critical Thinking | 10 marks | Analysis, evaluation, application, not just description |

> [!warning] Key Rule
> Critical thinking is worth **10 out of 22 marks**. Purely descriptive answers are capped. You must evaluate, not just describe.

---

## ERQ Command Terms

> [!note] Command Terms
>
> **Discuss:** Present evidence and arguments for and against; weigh them up and reach a balanced conclusion. Most common command term on Paper 2.
>
> **Evaluate:** Make a judgement about the value or limitations of something using evidence. Requires a supported conclusion.
>
> **Contrast:** Explicitly point out differences between two or more things. Do not describe similarities.
>
> **To what extent:** Weigh up evidence and argue a clear position. Requires a definitive conclusion.

---

## ERQ Essay Structure

> [!tip] Essay Structure
>
> **1. Introduction:** Define key terms. State your thesis/argument clearly. Signpost what the essay will cover.
>
> **2. Study 1:** Describe aim, participants, procedure, findings, and conclusion. Link explicitly to the question.
>
> **3. Specific Critical Thinking (CT):** Evaluate Study 1: methodological strengths/limitations, ethical considerations, sample issues.
>
> **4. Study 2:** Second study, similarly described and linked to the essay question.
>
> **5. Holistic Critical Thinking (CT):** Evaluate the broader body of evidence: conflicting studies, cultural bias, ethical limitations of the field, generalisability.
>
> **6. Conclusion:** Directly answer the question. Summarise the weight of evidence. Return to your thesis.

- Aim for **700-900 words**
- Critical thinking must be substantive. Vague comments like "the study lacked ecological validity" without explanation will not earn marks.
- Every paragraph should visibly link back to the question.

## Types of Critical Thinking

### Specific CT: evaluating an individual study

- **Sample bias:** Was the sample representative? (WEIRD: Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, Democratic)
- **Methodology:** Lab experiment vs field study? Controlled but low ecological validity vs ecologically valid but less controlled?
- **Replication:** Has the study been replicated? Does it have high test-retest reliability?
- **Ethics:** Deception, lack of informed consent, psychological harm, right to withdraw?
- **Demand characteristics:** Did participants guess the study's aim and change their behaviour?

### Holistic CT: evaluating the body of evidence

- **Cultural bias:** Do findings apply universally, or are they culture-specific? (e.g. collectivist vs individualist societies)
- **Gender bias:** Were male/female samples equivalent? Are findings generalised inappropriately?
- **Conflicting evidence:** Are there studies that contradict the claim? What does the balance of evidence suggest?
- **Ethical limitations:** Has the field been constrained by ethical guidelines, limiting what can be studied?
- **Reductionism:** Does the explanation oversimplify complex behaviour by reducing it to one factor?

## Key Studies by Subtopic (Human Relationships)

### 1. Personal Relationships

| Topic | Key Studies | Key Terms |
|---|---|---|
| Formation of relationships / attraction | [[Markey & Markey (2007)]], [[Hazan & Shaver (1987)]], [[Buss (1989)]] | Proximity, similarity, physical attractiveness, reciprocal liking |
| Evolutionary explanations for attraction | [[Buss (1989)]], [[Fisher et al 2005]], [[Wedekind (1995)]], [[Zhou et al (2014) - Boar AND & EST]] | Sexual selection, parental investment, reproductive fitness, gender differences in mate choice |
| Romantic love | [[Fisher et al 2005]] (dopamine and attachment); [[Hazan & Shaver (1987)]] (attachment styles) | Passionate love, companionate love, attachment, internal working model |
| Relationship maintenance and breakdown | [[Rusbult (1983)]] (investment model); [[Gottman (1999)]] (four horsemen) | Commitment, satisfaction, quality of alternatives, stonewalling, contempt |

### 2. Group Dynamics

| Topic | Key Studies | Key Terms |
|---|---|---|
| Cooperation and competition | [[Sherif et al (1954) - Robbers cave]] | Realistic conflict theory, superordinate goals, intergroup conflict |
| Conflict resolution | [[Sherif et al (1954) - Robbers cave]]; [[Aronson et al. (1978)]] (jigsaw classroom) | Contact hypothesis, cooperative learning, equal status contact |
| Communication in relationships | [[Gottman (1999)]], [[Bradbury & Fincham (1992) - Relationship enhancing patterns]] | Four horsemen, demand-withdrawal, responsibility attributions |

### 3. Social Responsibility

| Topic | Key Studies | Key Terms |
|---|---|---|
| Bystanderism | [[Darley & Latané (1968)]], [[Piliavin et al. (1969)]] | Diffusion of responsibility, pluralistic ignorance, evaluation apprehension, arousal-cost-reward model |
| Promoting prosocial behaviour | [[Piliavin et al. (1969)]], [[Batson et al. (1981)]], [[Darley & Batson (1973) - Good samaritan]] | Empathy-altruism hypothesis, negative state relief, kin selection |
| Prejudice and discrimination | [[Tajfel (1971) - Klee or Kandinsky]], [[Sherif et al (1954) - Robbers cave]], [[Harris & Fiske (2006)]] | Social identity theory, in-group bias, realistic conflict theory, stereotype content model |
| Reducing prejudice | [[Sherif et al (1954) - Robbers cave]], [[Aronson et al. (1978)]] | Superordinate goals, jigsaw classroom, contact hypothesis |
