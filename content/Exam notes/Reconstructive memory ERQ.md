# ERQ trial #1

    To what extent is memory reliable? 

Although memory is vital for humans and it can be very efficient (Depending on context), it is also unreliable. We rely on memory for almost every action we do, including complex information processing. Through the processes in memory we learn how to be "ourselves". We are constantly gathering information from our surroundings through "Attention", for it to be "Encoded" and stored to be recalled when needed in the future. However is this constant activation on the different processes what makes it malleable and unreliable. Psychologists suggest that memory is in fact **reconstructive**; memories are not stored as a whole but rather 'pieced together' every time we recall. In extension, this allows for distortion or the addition of new information. Loftus & Palmer (1974), suggested  that post-event information  can be added if it is presented in a credible manner and from a trusted source. Furthermore, Loftus & Prickrell (1995) showed that false memories can be implanted.  In this essay how these findings affect the reliability of memory is going to be discussed.

Loftus and palmer (1974), performed an experiment to study the influence of leading questions in eyewitness memories. 45  learning drivers were divided into 5 groups and shown 7  traffic accidents from driver's education films. The study used independent sampling, meaning each participant was exposed to only one condition. Participants were asked about how fast the cars were going when they hit each other through a questionnaire. The independent variable was chaining the verb "hit" for more or less complementary verbs like "smashed" or "contacted". 

Findings showed that the groups under the condition  with the more complementary verb (e.g. smashed) on average gave higher speed estimates than those under the conditions with the least complementary verb (e.g. contacted). indicating that the "critical word" in the question consistently activated schemas, which may have influenced the memory of the accident. This distortion of memory could be attributed to its reconstructive nature, so that the actual details of the accident are not remembered but when the schema is activated by the critical verb, what aligns with the schema overwrites the memory, affecting how the participants perceived the crash. Making the memory  inaccurate and unreliable.

Eventhough this study can be considered to have high internal validity as it uses a controlled lab experiment, the fact that participants were unexperienced drivers may signify am issue with ecological validity. Moreover, as the accidents were showed through educational films, participants may not experience the same level of emotion as if it were a real accident, reducing their personal relevance, thus possibly affecting the level of attention and recall of details.

Loftus & Prickrell (1995) carried out an experiment with the aim of determining if false memories of autobiographical events can be implanted through suggestion, suggesting memory could be transactive (we rely on others or our environment to remember). Prior to the actual participant testing, families were asked about the participant's childhood and wether if they had been lost in a mall or not. Participants were sent a questionnaire in which they were asked to recall 3 real memories and a false one (been last in a mall). Through the course of 4 weeks, participants were interviewed twice and asked to recall details of the 4 memories. In the second interview they  were debriefed, asked to rate their confidence in the memories and to guess the false memory.

25% of the participants recalled the false memory as real, although ranked it as least confident. Supporting the theory that when information comes from a trusted or credible source (family in this case) as is assimilated through repetition, false memories can be implanted, and the more practice done; the more details and confidence can be added to the memory, making it inaccurate and unreliable. Additionally, it supports the idea that memory is social (transactive) as it can be influenced by other people or external factors.

In contrast with Loftus & Palmer. As part of this study was performed via questionnaire in the participant's natural environment, Loftus & prickrell could be argued to have high ecological validity. Nonetheless this fact could also signify contamination of the responses on the questionnaire due to lack of control (Bias). Both studies could be seen as reliable as they are reasonably easy to replicate. 

Research into memory is trust worthy as accuracy, efficiency and confidence of memory can be easily tested, however some controls are needed, for instance, verifying details with the families. Additionally, method triangulation such as using both the questionnaire and interviews, can increase the credibility of the study. Experiments on memory can help the design of models, test theory and establish cause and effect relationships such as the effect of leading questions in memory perception.

Although memory is vital for planning and  decision making and it is generally efficient, it is also malleable making it highly unreliable and inaccurate at times.  Memory's reconstructive process can lead to extraneous or inaccurate details or even complete memories to be implanted, distorting the memory, making it eyewitness testimonies hardly a fiable source. Both social or environmental factors and cognitive heuristics like schemas can cause distortions in memory.
