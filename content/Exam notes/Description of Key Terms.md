[[Heuristics - Schema Theory]]: 
- **Schemas** are mental representations of knowledge derived from previous experience and knowledge that are stored in the brain. Schemas are used to speed up responses in daily activities. 
	 - Network or knowledge, beliefs and expectations
	 - **Influence on:** Perception, Decision making (DM) and Memory
- **Heuristics** (often used in DM or judgment formation as a 'rule'), are <u>mental shortcuts</u> that involve focusing on one aspect of a complex problem and ignoring others

**Cognitive Misery**: We <u>tend</u> to use heuristics because of 3 factors;
	Knowledge, motivation and economy. 
we make the choice not to actively process information to save time and effort.
	 Avoid deep processing (usually in mundane/routine tasks) -> save cognitive resources.

[[Cognitive Dissonance]]: 
Real psychological discomfort experienced when holding conflicting beliefs with regards to attitudes. 

Often when inconsistencies present between someones behaviour and beliefs leading to them changing either one to eliminate discomfort.
	e.g., behaviour is conflicting with beliefs
	1. change beliefs to justify behavior
	2. change behavior to fit behaviour 

[[Conformity]]: "*Social animals with a basic need to belong*"
When an individual adapts their behaviour to be in line with others. 
Often determined by <u>social comparison</u> and <u>normative social influence</u>. 

[[Social Identity Theory]]: 
	(Influence of a group membership on self-concept and behaviour)

A person has not just one "*personal self*" but, rather *several social selves* that correspond to group memberships. 

SIT predicts that when one of our social identities becomes <u>salient</u>, it will have an influence on our behaviour. 

[[Localization of function]]: 
Theory that specific parts of the brain are responsible for specific behaviors or cognitive processes. 

[[Neuroplasticity]]: 
	(High stimuli & learning opportunities increase density of neural connections)

Our brain's ability to make new connections,  pathways, and change how its circuits are wired to adapt to environmental/body changes/demands. 
	- **Dendric Branching:** When we learn something, neurons connect to make a new 'trace' in the brain. Dendrites grow in number and connect with other neurons. 
	- **Synaptic Pruning**: When a <u>synapse</u> is left under-stimulated, it is believed that this is the way in which our brain removes them for efficiency purposes. This process is not fully understood as of 16/03/2026.
