## **Evolutionary Theory of Mating Behavior**

This is the **main theoretical framework** for interpreting the study.

It explains human attraction and mating preferences as **adaptations** shaped by natural selection to maximize reproductive success.

  

### **🔹** **Parental Investment Theory ([[Trivers, 1972]]):**

- **Core idea:**
    
    The sex that invests more in offspring (gestation, lactation, nurturing) will be more selective about mates.
    
    - **Females**: High biological investment (pregnancy, time, energy) → must be selective → seek mates with resources, protection, and commitment.
        
    - **Males**: Low minimum biological investment (sperm) → less selective → can maximize fitness by pursuing multiple partners.
        
    

**Clark & Hatfield link:**

- **Men**’s high acceptance of casual sex supports the idea of a short-term mating strategy: maximize number of partners to increase reproductive success.
    
- **Women**’s near-zero acceptance supports the idea of a long-term strategy: avoid risky short-term mating because of pregnancy risk and potential social costs.

### **🔹** **Sexual Strategies Theory ([[Buss & Schmitt, 1993]]):**

- Suggests men and women both use **short-term and long-term mating strategies**, but in different ways depending on context.
    
- Men are generally more inclined to **short-term mating** if no constraints exist; women are more inclined to **long-term** unless resources and safety are guaranteed.

## Cognitive Theories of Attraction**

Although evolutionary theory explains the biological side, **cognitive factors** also shape sexual decision-making:

### **🔹** **Schema Theory (Bartlett, 1932):**

- People develop **schemas** about what is “appropriate” behavior for men and women in sexual situations based on cultural norms and media.
    
- Women may have internalized schemas linking casual sex to **risk, reputation loss, or stigma**, whereas men may have schemas linking casual sex to **status or opportunity**.

**Clark & Hatfield link:**

- The study took place in **1980s USA**, a time when social stigma for female sexuality was higher. Women’s schemas would discourage them from saying yes.
    
### **🔹** **Cost–Benefit Analysis (Cognitive Decision-Making Models):**

- People weigh **risks and rewards** before making decisions.
    
- For women, the potential cost of casual sex (pregnancy, reputation, safety) outweighs the reward.
    
- For men, the perceived costs are much lower, so the benefit dominates the decision.
    

---

## Sociocultural Theories (Gender Role Theory)**

  

### **🔹** **Social Role Theory (Eagly & Wood, 1999):**

- Gender differences in behavior are not just biological but also **socially constructed**.
    
- Cultural norms and gender roles shape expectations:
    
    - Men are expected to be sexually assertive.
        
    - Women are expected to be sexually reserved.
        
    

  

**Clark & Hatfield link:**

- In the 1980s, women saying “yes” to casual sex could face heavy social judgment. This cultural norm may explain the 0% female acceptance rate more than pure biology.

This suggests that human mating behavior is best understood through an interactionist perspective.”
