#PSY #HRelations
___

The **Psychology of Human Relationships** is an **applied option assessed in Paper 2** of IB SL Psychology, alongside Abnormal Psychology. You answer questions on your chosen option only. It examines personal relationships, social responsibility, and group dynamics through biological, cognitive, and sociocultural lenses.

---

## The Three Topics

| Topic | Key Questions |
|---|---|
| **Personal Relationships** | How and why do relationships form? How are they maintained? Why do they change or end? |
| **Social Responsibility** | Why do people help or fail to help others? What promotes prosocial behaviour? |
| **Group Dynamics** | How do groups form? Why does prejudice and discrimination occur? How does conflict arise and resolve? |

## Topic 1: Personal Relationships

### Formation of Relationships

Relationships form through a combination of biological, cognitive, and sociocultural factors.

**Biological approach:**
- **Fisher et al. (2005)**: romantic love activates the dopaminergic reward pathway (VTA, caudate nucleus). Love is a motivational drive, not an emotion.
- **Wedekind (1995)**: women prefer the scent of MHC-dissimilar men, supporting an evolved mechanism for genetic compatibility in mate selection.
- **Buss (1989)**: universal sex differences in mate preferences across 37 cultures support evolutionary (parental investment) explanations.

**Cognitive approach:**
- **Hazan & Shaver (1987)**: adult romantic attachment styles (secure, avoidant, anxious-ambivalent) mirror childhood attachment patterns, mediated by the internal working model.
- **Markey & Markey (2007)**: people seek partners similar to themselves; some complementarity in dominance is associated with greater relationship satisfaction.

**Sociocultural approach:**
- **Clark & Hatfield (1989)**: gender differences in receptivity to casual sex reflect social norms and evolutionary pressures; 75% of men vs. 0% of women agreed to a stranger's sexual proposition.
- **Ronay & von Hippel (2010)**: presence of an attractive female observer raised testosterone and increased risk-taking in young men, demonstrating mate display behaviour.

---

### Communication in Relationships

- **Gottman (1999)**: the Four Horsemen (criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling) predict divorce. Contempt is the strongest predictor. Studied 124 newlywed couples over 6 years using SPAFF coding and physiological measures. Notably found the Active Listening Model was **not** supported.
- **Bradbury & Fincham (1992)**: attributional patterns predict satisfaction. Happy couples make relationship-enhancing attributions (situational for bad, personal for good). Unhappy couples show distress-maintaining patterns. Used 47 couples, observation + questionnaire, three coders.

---

### Change and Dissolution

- **Gottman (1999)**: communication patterns in year 1 predict divorce years later. 17 divorces occurred in the sample after 6 years.
- **Felmlee (1995)**: fatal attractions: in 88/301 cases, the initially attractive quality became the primary reason for the breakup (fun becomes foolish, strong becomes domineering, spontaneous becomes unpredictable).

## Topic 2: Social Responsibility

### Bystanderism and Prosocial Behaviour

- **Piliavin et al. (1969)**: Arousal: Cost-Reward Model. On the NYC subway, a cane victim received help on 95% of trials; a drunk victim on 50%. Group size did not reduce helping in face-to-face emergencies, directly challenging diffusion of responsibility.
- **Darley & Batson (1973)**: time pressure overrode moral values; only 10% of seminary students in a hurry helped a slumped confederate, even after preparing a Good Samaritan speech.
- **Drury et al. (2009)**: shared social identity increased helping in a VR emergency simulation. Group identification, not individual disposition, determines prosocial behaviour in emergencies.

---

## Topic 3: Group Dynamics

### Prejudice and Discrimination

- **Tajfel (1971)**: minimal group paradigm showed that social categorisation alone produces in-group favouritism and out-group discrimination, even with arbitrary meaningless groups.
- **Sherif et al. (1954)**: competition produces intergroup hostility; superordinate goals reduce it. Contact alone did not reduce prejudice.
- **Steele & Aronson (1995)**: stereotype threat impaired Black students' test performance under diagnostic framing but not non-diagnostic framing. Mechanism: threat consumes working memory resources.

### Cooperation and Conflict

- **Sherif et al. (1954)**: realistic conflict theory: competition over scarce resources produces prejudice; cooperation toward superordinate goals reduces it.
- **Drury et al. (2009)**: shared identity promotes cooperation in emergencies.

## IB Exam Guidance

**Paper 2 format:** Same as Paper 1 — Section A (SAQ, 9 marks each) and Section B (ERQ, 22 marks). All questions are from Human Relationships only.

**For SAQs:** Pick one approach per question. Describe one study in APPR format and link it explicitly to the question.

**For ERQs:** Use 2-3 studies. Evaluate each one. Add holistic CT (e.g., cross-cultural limitations, ethical concerns, reductionism of biological accounts).

**High-value contrast for ERQs:** biological vs. cognitive approach to relationship formation (Fisher vs. Hazan & Shaver), or situational vs. dispositional explanations of prosocial behaviour (Piliavin vs. Darley & Batson).
