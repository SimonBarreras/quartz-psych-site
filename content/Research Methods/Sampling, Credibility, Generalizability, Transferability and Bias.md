**Sampling**

>[!Note]
>A **Sample** is the group of individuals taking part in the study. **Sampling**, is the process of finding and recruiting the individuals 

When looking at a study and it is important to differentiate between:

- **Population:** The type of people whose behaviour we are interested in understanding
- **Sample:** A small portion/number of people from the population chosen to represent its totality
	 Your sample is limited if you study only a certain or significantly small group of people. (This would be **Sampling Bias**).
- **Sampling Methods:** The method used to select the *sample* from the *population*.
	  The sample techniques may affect the results according to the strengths and limitations of every method.

___
**Credibility** 

>[!Note]
>Degree to which results can be trusted to reflect reality. The results don't reflect reality if there was some sort of **bias** on the study.

e.g./ In an interview, participants may actually guess the aim of the study and vary their responses.

e.g.2/ Researchers themselves may selectively notice supporting evidence and unintentionally ignore contracting evidence.

Is there is indication that potential sources of bias were controlled or eliminated, credibility is believed to be high.

- Credibility can be increased using:
	- Triangulation (Method, Researcher or Different data sources)
	- Inductive Content Analysis (ICA)
	- Member checking
___
**Bias**: "*Systematic deviation from the 'truth'*"

In **experimental research** bias comes in the form of confounding factors that may influence cause and effect relationships decreasing internal validity.

In **correlational studies** can occur in the level of variable measurement and level of interpretation of findings.

>[!Note]
>Biasses are systematic deviations agains something or someone that can affect the results.
>
>Refers to the extent to which results reflect reality, if results do not reflect reality, there is *Bias* "traps"
>
>Approaches in quantitative and qualitative research are different.
