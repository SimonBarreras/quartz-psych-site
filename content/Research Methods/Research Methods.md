Within Quantitative research there are 3 main types of research.

- **Experimental studies**: Look at **Cause and effect inference**. Manipulation of *Independent variables* (IV's) and the effect of that manipulation over *Dependent Variables* (DV's). All other potentially important variables are controlled to the maximum and are known as *Controlled variables* (CV's)
- **[[Correlational Studies]]**: Looks at **Trends in Data**, though descriptive studies. There is no manipulation done by the researcher over any variables. Variables are observed and their relationship quantified. It is common to correlate variables though the use of formulas. Relationships are not investigated but rather approached separately.
- **[[Descriptive studies]]:** Looks at **independent variables** rather than relationships. They are used in both, Sociology and Psychology to conduct a **broad investigation** of a phenomenon before getting into the specifics. An common examples are [[Public opinion Surveys]]  where the distribution of answers to specific questions is measured.
