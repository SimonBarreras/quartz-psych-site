PSY Exam

Question 1: Always the same (methods and sampling)

1a. Method - Observation / survey / questionnaire / Focus groups / Interviews / Experiment / Case study

1b. Sampling - Opportunity / (Purposive) / Random / Volunteer / Snowball / Stratified

1c. Additional - Focus Group / Survey

Question 2: Ethics ( one of two is always asked)

2a/b Ethical considerations (CARDUD) - During study / On publishing - Anonymity - Reputation - Knock on effect - policy - funding

  1. Describe the ethical considerations that were applied and explain if further ethical considerations could be applied.
  2. Describe the ethical considerations in reporting results and explain ethical considerations that could be taken into account when applying the findings

1)All ethical considerations in the text should be described and other considerations should be addressed 2)2 Parts, describing reporting of the results. It is important to consider anonymity and communicating results effectively

  • 2nd hypothetical but could focus on its potential effects on different groups, leading; leading to stress or discrimination.

Question 3: Theoretical understanding (1 of 3 is always asked)

3a. Discuss the possibility of generalizing/transferring the findings of the study (transferability and Generalizability)

(“Refers to the extent to which the results can be applied beyond the sample and settings (sometimes in Quantitative research you want to generalize findings to a more broad population).“) -Statistical generalization (Experiments / Survey) - representative nature of the sample - Transferability of insight - “match” with another population

3b. Discuss how a researcher could ensure results are credible (credibility) (“Refers to the extent to which the results of the study can be trusted.“) - Reliability - Details - Triangulation

3c. Discuss how a researcher could avoid bias (bias)

(“Refers to the extent to which results reflect reality, if results do not reflect reality, there is  Bias “traps"") - Bias can come form researcher, Method (Res), Sample Method, Participants (E.g. modification of responses for personal benefit, Social desirability)

Reminder

  • Must make direct reference to the stimulus piece
  • In quantitative research, Generalizability, has a lot to do with the representative nature of the sample, size and ecological validity.
  • In qualitative focus is in the transferable nature of the sample.
  • Ways to ensure credibility include, Method triangulation, Researcher triangulation, Data triangulation, feedback, between others.
  • Ways of avoiding bias in experimental research include the use of Blind controls, Sampling method, the role of reflexivity, and triangulation, between others.
  • Inductive Content Analysis (ICA), used to analyze and publish results from open-ended questionnaires, interviews or focus groups.

correlational studies

  • Quantitative approach to studying behaviour
  • Correlational studies should have no manipulation from researcher.
  • Must yield statistical results
  • Most common data collection through Questionnaires and surveys or Psychometric tests (e.g. IQ tests)
  • Regression Analysis
  • Inductive content analysis (no statistical measurement but interpreting anf drawing conclusions) (Does not qualify as “correlational study” although it might show correlation between variables).

General Specifications

  • Duration: 1 hour
  • Total Marks: 24 marks
  • Weighting: 20% (Higher Level only)
  • Format: Unseen research scenario → 3 compulsory questions (no choice)
  • Focus: Research methods in psychology — qualitative and quantitative

What Paper 3 Actually Tests

You will be given a research scenario you have never seen before and must answer all three questions about it. Success depends on knowing research methods deeply enough to apply them to any study — not memorising specific studies.

Question 1 — Research Methods & Sampling (9 marks)

Key Rule

Do not use bullet points for Q1. Write in full sentences. Each question is worth 3 marks: 1 mark for correct identification + 2 marks for two correct characteristics.

1a — Identify the research method + outline two characteristics (3 marks)

Identify the method used in the stimulus, then describe two of its defining characteristics in your own words. Always link to the stimulus where possible.

Research Methods to Know

MethodKey CharacteristicsQualitative or Quantitative?
Experiment (lab)Controlled environment; manipulation of IV; measurement of DV; random allocation possibleQuantitative
Experiment (field)Natural setting; manipulation of IV; higher ecological validity; less controlQuantitative
Observation (naturalistic)Behaviour observed in natural setting; no manipulation; high ecological validity; risk of observer biasQualitative / Quantitative
Observation (participant)Researcher joins the group being studied; rich insider data; risk of researcher biasQualitative
Structured interviewPre-set closed questions; standardised; allows comparison; less depthQuantitative

1b — Describe the sampling method used (3 marks)

Same structure: 1 mark for identifying the correct method + 2 marks for two characteristics. Connect to the stimulus (e.g. mention the sample size or group described).

Sampling Methods to Know

MethodHow it worksStrengthsLimitations
Opportunity / ConvenienceWhoever is available at the timeEasy and quickLikely biased; not representative
Volunteer / Self-selectedParticipants respond to an advert or requestEasy to recruit; willing participantsSelf-selection bias; not representative
PurposiveResearcher deliberately selects participants with specific characteristics relevant to the studyHighly relevant sampleResearcher bias in selection; not generalisable
SnowballExisting participants recruit further participants from their networkUseful for hard-to-reach groupsChain bias; not representative
RandomEvery member of the target population has an equal chance of being selectedMost representative methodDifficult and time-consuming in practice
StratifiedPopulation divided into subgroups; participants randomly selected from each in proportionRepresentative of subgroupsComplex to organise

1c — Suggest an additional (or alternative) research method + one reason (3 marks)

Strategy

Suggest an additional method rather than an alternative one — it is easier to justify adding a method to complement the existing one than to argue for replacing it entirely. Always justify your choice by connecting it to what the original study could not do (e.g. small sample, lack of depth, low ecological validity).

Example answer structure: “An additional research method would be [method]. This is because [reason linked to the stimulus — e.g. the original study had only 12 participants, so a survey would allow a larger sample and enable statistical generalisation of the findings].”

Question 2 — Ethical Considerations (6 marks)

Only one of the two variants below will appear — you will not get a choice.

You must discuss 6 ethical points in total (because the question is worth 6 marks).

Variant 2a — Ethics applied during the study

Question: Describe the ethical considerations that were applied in the study and explain if further ethical considerations could be applied.

  • Part 1: Identify and describe 3 ethical considerations that were actually used in the stimulus study. Quote or reference the stimulus directly.
  • Part 2: Identify and explain 3 additional ethical considerations that were NOT in the stimulus but could/should have been applied. Explain how they would be applied, not just what they are.

Variant 2b — Ethics in reporting and applying findings

Question: Describe the ethical considerations in reporting the results and explain ethical considerations that could be taken into account when applying the findings of the study.

  • Part 1: Describe how ethical considerations apply when publishing and reporting the results (e.g. anonymity, accurate representation of data).
  • Part 2: Explain ethical considerations for applying the findings — how might they affect different groups once published? Could they cause harm, discrimination, or be misused by media or policy?

Ethical Considerations Reference List (CARDUD +)

Aim to know at least 8–10 of these in depth

Ethical ConsiderationWhat it meansHow to apply it

Right to withdrawParticipants can leave at any time without penaltyMust be stated at the start; no pressure to continue

AnonymityParticipants cannot be identified from published resultsDo not include names, locations, or identifying details in reports

Question 3 — Theoretical Understanding (9 marks)

Only one of the three variants below will appear — you will not get a choice.

Must make direct and specific reference to the stimulus throughout your answer.

Variant 3a — Generalisation / Transferability

Question: Discuss the possibility of generalising / transferring the findings of the study.

Key Distinction

Whether you discuss generalisation (quantitative) or transferability (qualitative) depends on the type of study in the stimulus. Know both.

If the study is quantitative → discuss Generalisation

“Refers to the extent to which findings can be applied to a broader population beyond the sample.”

  • Sample size — larger samples increase likelihood of being representative
  • Sampling method — random/stratified sampling → more representative; opportunity/snowball → less
  • Demographics — is the sample WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, Democratic)? This limits generalisability
  • Ecological validity — were conditions artificial (lab) or realistic? Low ecological validity limits generalisation to real life
  • Replication — have findings been reproduced across different populations?

If the study is qualitative → discuss Transferability

“Refers to the extent to which insights from the study could be relevant or meaningful in another context or population.”

  • Thick description — detailed, rich accounts allow readers to judge whether findings fit their own context
  • Purposive sample — if selected for specific characteristics, findings may transfer to others with those characteristics
  • Context-specificity — qualitative findings are deeply tied to the specific setting; direct generalisation is not the goal
  • Theoretical generalisation — findings may extend existing theory even without statistical generalisation

Variant 3b — Credibility

Question: Discuss how a researcher could ensure that the results of the study are credible.

This question usually only appears when the stimulus study uses qualitative methods.

“Credibility refers to the extent to which the results of the study can be trusted — i.e. do they accurately represent the participants’ reality?”

StrategyWhat it involves
TriangulationUsing multiple methods, researchers, or data sources to cross-check findings
Method triangulationCombining qualitative and quantitative methods (e.g. interview + survey)
Researcher triangulationMultiple researchers independently analyse the same data and compare interpretations
Data triangulationCollecting data at different times, locations, or from different participant groups
Member checkingReturning findings to participants to verify they accurately reflect their experience
Prolonged engagementSpending extended time in the field to build trust and deepen understanding
Peer debriefingHaving colleagues scrutinise the researcher’s methods and interpretations
ReflexivityResearcher acknowledges and documents their own biases and how these may have shaped findings
Audit trailKeeping a detailed record of all research decisions so the process can be independently reviewed
Thick / rich descriptionProviding detailed accounts that allow readers to assess credibility themselves

Variant 3c — Avoiding Bias

Question: Discuss how a researcher could avoid bias in the study.

“Bias refers to any systematic distortion that prevents results from accurately reflecting reality.”

Sources of Bias

SourceType of BiasExample
Researcher / Experimenter biasResearcher’s expectations unconsciously influence resultsUnconsciously asking leading questions in an interview
Participant biasParticipants modify behaviour because they are being observedSocial desirability — giving answers they think are expected
Sampling biasSample does not represent the target populationUsing only university students (WEIRD sample)
Confirmation biasResearcher interprets data to confirm existing beliefsIgnoring contradictory qualitative data
Demand characteristicsParticipants guess the aim and alter their behaviourGuessing the hypothesis in an experiment

Ways to Avoid Bias

StrategyHow it reduces bias
Single-blind procedureParticipants do not know which condition they are in → reduces demand characteristics
Double-blind procedureNeither participants nor researcher know condition allocation → eliminates experimenter bias
Standardised proceduresSame instructions and conditions for all participants → reduces researcher variability
ReflexivityResearcher examines their own assumptions and documents them → reduces confirmation bias
TriangulationCross-checking findings across multiple methods/researchers → reduces single-source bias
Random allocationParticipants randomly assigned to conditions → reduces selection bias in experiments
Appropriate samplingUsing random or stratified sampling → reduces sample bias
Pilot studyTesting procedure in advance to identify and remove sources of bias before the main study
Inductive Content Analysis (ICA)Bottom-up coding of qualitative data without pre-set categories → reduces confirmation bias in analysis

ICA Note

Inductive Content Analysis is a qualitative analysis technique used to analyse interviews, focus groups, and open-ended questionnaires. Categories emerge from the data rather than being imposed by the researcher. It does not produce statistical results and does not qualify as a correlational study, even if patterns emerge.

Correlational Studies — Key Points

  • A quantitative approach — must yield statistical results (e.g. correlation coefficient)
  • No manipulation of variables by the researcher
  • Most common data collection methods: surveys, questionnaires, psychometric tests (e.g. IQ tests)
  • Analysis: Pearson’s r, Spearman’s rho, regression analysis
  • Cannot establish cause and effect — only association between variables
  • Inductive Content Analysis does not qualify as a correlational study — it produces no statistical measure of correlation

Qualitative vs Quantitative — Quick Reference

FeatureQualitativeQuantitative
Data typeWords, themes, meaningsNumbers, statistics
GoalUnderstand experience / behaviour in depthMeasure, compare, generalise
Common methodsInterviews, focus groups, case studies, naturalistic observationExperiments, surveys, correlational studies
AnalysisInductive Content Analysis, thematic analysisStatistical analysis, regression
GeneralisationTransferability of insightStatistical generalisation
StrengthRich, contextual, participant-centredReplicable, objective, comparable
LimitationHard to generalise; researcher bias riskLacks depth; may miss context

Exam Tips

Time Management

TimeTask
0–10 minRead stimulus carefully. Answer Q1a, Q1b, Q1c.
10–30 minQ2 — ethical considerations (6 points, ~3 min each)
30–55 minQ3 — generalisation / credibility / bias
55–60 minReview — check all answers reference the stimulus

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using bullet points in Q1 — write in full sentences
  • Only identifying the method/sampling without giving two characteristics
  • Suggesting an alternative rather than an additional method in Q1c (harder to justify)
  • Listing ethical considerations without explaining how they apply to the specific study
  • Writing generic answers for Q3 — every point must connect back to the stimulus
  • Confusing generalisation (quantitative) with transferability (qualitative)
  • Treating ICA as a correlational method

What Examiners Reward

  • Correct identification + two clearly explained characteristics (Q1)
  • Direct reference to the stimulus throughout all answers
  • Six distinct, well-explained ethical points in Q2
  • Correct application of generalisation vs transferability based on study type (Q3a)
  • Concrete strategies explained with their mechanism — not just named (Q3b/c)
  • Terminology used accurately and consistently
